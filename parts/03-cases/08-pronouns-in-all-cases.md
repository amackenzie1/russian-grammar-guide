# Pronouns and determiners in all cases

Over the last six sections you've collected pronoun forms one case at a time: меня́ here, мне there, со мной somewhere else. This section puts them all on one page, adds the words that behave like them (э́тот, тот, весь, the possessives, кто and что), and sorts out the small rules that govern how they combine with prepositions.

These are short words, but they are the most frequent case forms in the language. In real conversation, people are "me", "you" and "him" far more often than they are nouns. If you learn only one table in this book by heart, make it the first one below.

:::vocab
себя́ | oneself | no nominative
весь, вся, всё, все | all, the whole; everything, everyone |
тот, та, то, те | that, those |
оди́н, одна́, одно́, одни́ | one; alone; a certain |
чей, чья, чьё, чьи | whose |
всё | everything | n. of весь
все | everyone | pl. of весь
поэ́тому | so, that's why |
дела́ | things, affairs | pl. of де́ло
:::

## Personal pronouns

| Case | я | ты | он, оно́ | она́ | мы | вы | они́ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | я | ты | он, оно́ | она́ | мы | вы | они́ |
| Accusative | меня́ | тебя́ | его́ | её | нас | вас | их |
| Genitive | меня́ | тебя́ | его́ | её | нас | вас | их |
| Dative | мне | тебе́ | ему́ | ей | нам | вам | им |
| Instrumental | мной | тобо́й | им | ей | на́ми | ва́ми | и́ми |
| Prepositional | мне | тебе́ | нём | ней | нас | вас | них |

Some things that make this smaller than it looks:

- The accusative and genitive are **always identical**. Pronouns refer to people so often that the "animate accusative = genitive" rule has simply become their only pattern.
- Он and оно́ share all their forms outside the nominative.
- For я and ты, the dative and prepositional coincide: мне, тебе́.
- Мы and вы are perfectly parallel: нас / вас, нам / вам, на́ми / ва́ми.

And some overlaps to be aware of, since they can cause a double take when reading:

- **им** is both "by him" (instrumental of он) and "to them" (dative of они́).
- **ей** is both "to her" (dative) and "by her" (instrumental).
- **его́, её, их** are both object pronouns (him, her, them) and the unchanging possessives (his, her, their).

:::ex
Я ему́ не ве́рю. | I don't believe him.
Я им не ве́рю. | I don't believe them.
Мы им горди́мся. | We're proud of him.
Я зна́ю её. Я зна́ю её бра́та. | I know her. I know her brother.
:::

## The н- rule

Whenever a third-person pronoun comes directly after a preposition, it gets an **н-** at the front. You've seen this in every case section; here is the full set.

| Case | Without preposition | With preposition |
|---|---|---|
| Accusative | его́, её, их | на него́, на неё, на них |
| Genitive | его́, её, их | у него́, у неё, у них |
| Dative | ему́, ей, им | к нему́, к ней, к ним |
| Instrumental | им, ей, и́ми | с ним, с ней, с ни́ми |
| Prepositional | — | о нём, о ней, о них |

The prepositional never occurs without a preposition, which is why its forms are listed with н- in the main table.

:::ex
Я ви́жу его́. Я смотрю́ на него́. | I see him. I'm looking at him.
Я звоню́ ей. Я иду́ к ней. | I'm calling her. I'm going to her place.
Мы горди́мся и́ми. Мы рабо́таем с ни́ми. | We're proud of them. We work with them.
У них есть де́ти. | They have children.
Э́то пода́рок для неё. | This is a present for her.
Без него́ ску́чно. | It's boring without him.
:::

The н- has no meaning; it's a historical leftover that makes the combination easier to pronounce.

:::warn У него́ or у его́?
The н- attaches only when его́, её, их are *pronouns*, the object of the preposition themselves. When they are *possessives* ("his", "her", "their") modifying a following noun, they never change, н- included.

- У **него́** есть маши́на. (He has a car.)
- У **его́** бра́та есть маши́на. (His brother has a car.)
- Я был у **неё**. (I was at her place.)
- Я был у **её** ма́мы. (I was at her mom's.)
- Письмо́ от **них**. (A letter from them.)
- Письмо́ от **их** сы́на. (A letter from their son.)
:::

A few prepositions that began life as other kinds of words don't trigger the н-. The main one you know is благодаря́: благодаря́ ему́, благодаря́ ей, благодаря́ им.

## Prepositions that grow a vowel

Several short prepositions add an **о** before мне and мной, to avoid a pile-up of consonants.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| к + мне | ко мне | to me, to my place |
| с + мной | со мной | with me |
| в + мне | во мне | in me |
| о + мне | [[ns:обо]] мне | about me |
| пе́ред + мной | [[ns:передо]] мной | in front of me |
| над + мной | [[ns:надо]] мной | above me |

The same thing happens before the forms of весь that begin with вс-: ко всем (to everyone), со все́ми (with everyone), во всём (in everything), [[ns:обо]] всём (about everything).

:::ex
Сего́дня ве́чером все иду́т ко мне. | Everyone's coming over to my place tonight.
Ты идёшь со мной? | Are you coming with me?
Что ты зна́ешь [[ns:обо]] мне? | What do you know about me?
Он согла́сен со все́ми. | He agrees with everyone.
:::

## Себя́

Russian has one more personal pronoun, **себя́**, meaning "oneself". It refers back to the subject of the sentence, whoever that is, so it covers myself, yourself, himself, herself, ourselves and themselves. It has no nominative (it can never be the subject), and its other forms copy тебя́:

| Accusative, Genitive | Dative, Prepositional | Instrumental |
|---|---|---|
| себя́ | себе́ | собо́й |

:::ex
Я купи́л себе́ но́вый телефо́н. | I bought myself a new phone.
Как ты себя́ чу́вствуешь? | How are you feeling? | literally "how do you feel yourself?"
У тебя́ есть с собо́й па́спорт? | Do you have your passport on you? | literally "with yourself"
:::

It's covered properly, together with the possessive свой (one's own), in [one's own, oneself, each other](#s-06-06).

## Кто and что

The question words, which you met in the very first section of this part, decline like this:

| Case | кто | что |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | кто | что |
| Accusative | кого́ | что |
| Genitive | кого́ | чего́ |
| Dative | кому́ | чему́ |
| Instrumental | кем | чем |
| Prepositional | (о) ком | (о) чём |

Кто is animate, so its accusative equals its genitive. Что is inanimate, so its accusative equals its nominative. Just like nouns.

For agreement, кто counts as masculine singular and что as neuter singular, no matter who or what the answer turns out to be:

:::ex
Кто звони́л? — Ма́ша. | Who called? — Masha.
Что бы́ло в су́мке? | What was in the bag?
:::

A preposition always comes *before* the question word. English lets it dangle at the end ("Who are you talking *to*?"); Russian never does.

:::ex
С кем ты говори́л? | Who were you talking to?
О чём ты ду́маешь? | What are you thinking about?
У кого́ есть ру́чка? | Who has a pen?
К кому́ вы идёте? | Whose place are you going to?
Для кого́ э́ти цветы́? | Who are these flowers for?
Чем я могу́ вам помо́чь? | How can I help you? | literally "with what"
От чего́ э́тот ключ? | What is this key for?
:::

## Э́тот and тот

Э́тот (this) and тот (that) decline almost like adjectives, with a couple of short forms in the nominative and accusative.

| Case | m. / n. | f. | pl. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | э́тот / э́то | э́та | э́ти |
| Accusative | nom. or gen. / э́то | э́ту | nom. or gen. |
| Genitive | э́того | э́той | э́тих |
| Dative | э́тому | э́той | э́тим |
| Instrumental | э́тим | э́той | э́тими |
| Prepositional | э́том | э́той | э́тих |

"Nom. or gen." is the animate rule once more: э́тот стол but э́того челове́ка; э́ти столы́ but э́тих люде́й.

| Case | m. / n. | f. | pl. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | тот / то | та | те |
| Accusative | nom. or gen. / то | ту | nom. or gen. |
| Genitive | того́ | той | тех |
| Dative | тому́ | той | тем |
| Instrumental | тем | той | те́ми |
| Prepositional | том | той | тех |

Тот has **е** wherever э́тот has и: тем, тех, те́ми against э́тим, э́тих, э́тими.

:::ex
Я не зна́ю э́того челове́ка. | I don't know this person.
Мы живём в том до́ме. | We live in that building.
Что ты ду́маешь об э́тих фи́льмах? | What do you think of these movies?
В то вре́мя мы жи́ли в Каза́ни. | At that time we lived in Kazan.
С тех пор я его́ не ви́дел. | I haven't seen him since then. | "since those times"
:::

The neuter form **э́то**, used alone, is the all-purpose "this, that, it" for situations and ideas, and it declines too. This is not the unchanging э́то of Э́то мой брат; it's a real pronoun doing a job in the sentence.

:::ex
Я ма́ло зна́ю об э́том. | I don't know much about that.
По́сле э́того мы пи́ли чай. | After that we had tea.
Я не для э́того здесь. | That's not what I'm here for.
Из-за э́того я не спал всю ночь. | Because of that I didn't sleep all night.
:::

## Весь

Весь means "all" or "the whole", and its neuter and plural forms double as the pronouns **всё** (everything) and **все** (everyone).

| Case | m. / n. | f. | pl. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | весь / всё | вся | все |
| Accusative | nom. or gen. / всё | всю | nom. or gen. |
| Genitive | всего́ | всей | всех |
| Dative | всему́ | всей | всем |
| Instrumental | всем | всей | все́ми |
| Prepositional | всём | всей | всех |

Watch the difference between всем (instrumental singular, or dative plural) and всём (prepositional singular), and between всё (everything) and все (everyone). In ordinary Russian print the dots on ё are often left off, and you have to work out from the context which is meant.

:::ex
Я весь день был до́ма. | I was home all day.
Она́ всю жизнь живёт в Москве́. | She's lived in Moscow all her life.
Я всё по́нял. | I understood everything.
Все зна́ют об э́том. | Everyone knows about it.
Спаси́бо за всё. | Thanks for everything.
Я всех здесь зна́ю. | I know everyone here.
Он всем помога́ет. | He helps everyone.
Мы говори́ли [[ns:обо]] всём. | We talked about everything.
:::

Весь is hiding inside a number of common phrases: всё вре́мя (all the time), всё равно́ (all the same, anyway), всё в поря́дке (everything's fine), бо́льше всего́ (most of all), пре́жде всего́ (first of all), Всего́ хоро́шего! (All the best!).

## The possessives

Мой and наш are the two models. **Твой** and **свой** decline exactly like мой; **ваш** declines exactly like наш.

| Case | m. / n. | f. | pl. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | мой / моё | моя́ | мои́ |
| Accusative | nom. or gen. / моё | мою́ | nom. or gen. |
| Genitive | моего́ | мое́й | мои́х |
| Dative | моему́ | мое́й | мои́м |
| Instrumental | мои́м | мое́й | мои́ми |
| Prepositional | моём | мое́й | мои́х |

| Case | m. / n. | f. | pl. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | наш / на́ше | на́ша | на́ши |
| Accusative | nom. or gen. / на́ше | на́шу | nom. or gen. |
| Genitive | на́шего | на́шей | на́ших |
| Dative | на́шему | на́шей | на́шим |
| Instrumental | на́шим | на́шей | на́шими |
| Prepositional | на́шем | на́шей | на́ших |

Apart from the nominative and the feminine accusative, these are the soft adjective endings.

And once more, because it's a relief: **его́, её and их never change.** They are the same in every case, gender and number.

:::ex
Она́ рабо́тает с мои́м бра́том и с его́ жено́й. | She works with my brother and his wife.
Мы бы́ли у на́ших друзе́й и у их роди́телей. | We were at our friends' and at their parents'.
Она́ пи́шет о твое́й сестре́ и о её де́тях. | She's writing about your sister and her children.
:::

### Чей

Чей (whose) declines too, though outside the nominative you'll meet it mainly in writing. All its forms keep the soft sign: чьего́, чьему́, чьим, чьём in the masculine and neuter; чью, чьей in the feminine; чьих, чьим, чьи́ми in the plural.

:::ex
Чью кни́гу ты чита́ешь? | Whose book are you reading?
В чьей маши́не мы е́дем? | Whose car are we going in?
:::

## Оди́н

The number "one" is really a determiner, and it declines like э́тот, with the stress on the ending. It drops its и as soon as an ending is added.

| Case | m. / n. | f. | pl. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | оди́н / одно́ | одна́ | одни́ |
| Accusative | nom. or gen. / одно́ | одну́ | nom. or gen. |
| Genitive | одного́ | одно́й | одни́х |
| Dative | одному́ | одно́й | одни́м |
| Instrumental | одни́м | одно́й | одни́ми |
| Prepositional | одно́м | одно́й | одни́х |

Besides counting, оди́н means "alone" and "a certain, some". The plural одни́ means "alone" (of several people) or "nothing but".

:::ex
У меня́ то́лько одна́ сестра́. | I only have one sister.
Я живу́ оди́н. | I live alone. | a woman says одна́
Мы рабо́таем в одно́м о́фисе. | We work in the same office.
Я чита́л об э́том в одно́й кни́ге. | I read about it in a book (a certain book).
Оди́н мой друг живёт в Япо́нии. | A friend of mine lives in Japan.
:::

Other determiners that follow adjective patterns, such as сам (oneself) and са́мый (the very, the most), come up in [Part 6](#s-06-06). Complete tables are in [adjective, pronoun and determiner tables](#s-09-02).

:::aside Pronouns hiding inside other words
Once you know these forms, a lot of everyday vocabulary turns out to be a preposition glued to a pronoun. **Почему́** (why) is по + чему́, "on account of what?". **Потому́** in потому́ что (because) is по + тому́, "on account of that". **Поэ́тому** (so, that's why) is по + э́тому. **Заче́м** (what for) is за + чем. **Совсе́м** (completely) is со + всем. And the opinion words **по-мо́ему** and **по-тво́ему** (in my opinion, in your opinion) come from по + моему́, with a shifted stress.
:::

## Pronoun phrases worth knowing whole

Some combinations are so frequent that they work as units.

:::ex
у меня́, у тебя́, у нас | I have; at my place, at your place, at ours
Как у тебя́ дела́? — У меня́ всё хорошо́. | How are things with you? — Everything's good.
У нас так не говоря́т. | We don't say it that way (here). | "at ours they don't speak so"
ко мне, к нам | to my place, to our place
со мной, с тобо́й, с на́ми | with me, with you, with us
ме́жду на́ми | between us
Что с тобо́й? | What's the matter with you?
Э́то не для меня́. | It's not for me. / It's not my thing.
Мне всё равно́. | I don't care.
Тебе́ от неё приве́т. | She says hi. | literally "to you from her a hello"
Мне с тобо́й хорошо́. | I feel good when I'm with you.
:::

## All together now

Short pronouns pile up in real sentences, each carrying its own case. Try reading these and identifying the job of every pronoun before you look at the translation.

:::ex
Я говори́л с ним о тебе́. | I talked to him about you.
Она́ мно́го расска́зывала мне о вас. | She's told me a lot about you.
Она́ дала́ мне его́ но́мер, а я дал ему́ её а́дрес. | She gave me his number, and I gave him her address.
Ты идёшь с на́ми к ним? | Are you coming with us to their place?
О чём вы с ней говори́ли? | What were you and she talking about?
Без неё нам ску́чно. | We're bored without her.
Для него́ э́то о́чень ва́жно. | It's very important to him.
Кого́ из них ты зна́ешь? | Which of them do you know?
Я всем э́то говорю́, но мне не ве́рят. | I tell everyone that, but nobody believes me.
Она́ зна́ет [[ns:обо]] мне всё. | She knows everything about me.
Э́то всё, что у меня́ есть. | That's all I have.
:::

:::dialogue
А: Ты зна́ешь Ди́му и О́лю? | Do you know Dima and Olya?
Б: Его́ зна́ю, а её нет. А что? | I know him, but not her. Why?
А: Я вчера́ был у них. Они́ спра́шивали о тебе́. | I was at their place yesterday. They were asking about you.
Б: [[ns:Обо]] мне? Что и́менно? | About me? What exactly?
А: Ди́ма хо́чет рабо́тать с тобо́й. Ему́ нра́вится твой прое́кт. | Dima wants to work with you. He likes your project.
Б: Серьёзно? У тебя́ есть его́ но́мер? | Seriously? Do you have his number?
А: Есть. Но у него́ сейча́с мно́го рабо́ты. Тебе́ лу́чше позвони́ть ей: она́ всё зна́ет о его́ пла́нах. | I do. But he's got a lot of work right now. You'd better call her: she knows all about his plans.
:::
