# The imperative

The imperative is the form for telling people what to do: requests, invitations, instructions, advice, warnings. English just uses the bare verb ("wait!", "sit down"). Russian has a dedicated form, and, as you may have guessed by now, it has to pick an aspect. The aspect of a command carries a surprising amount of tone: the difference between a neutral request, a warm invitation, an impatient "get on with it" and a warning is often nothing but perfective versus imperfective.

:::vocab
закрыва́ть / закры́ть | to close | pf. закро́ю, закро́ешь
открыва́ть / откры́ть | to open | pf. откро́ю, откро́ешь
пока́зывать / показа́ть | to show | pf. покажу́, пока́жешь
передава́ть / переда́ть | to pass, to hand over
сади́ться / сесть | to sit down | pf. ся́ду, ся́дешь
проходи́ть / пройти́ | to come through, to go on in
па́дать / упа́сть | to fall | pf. упаду́, упадёшь
теря́ть / потеря́ть | to lose
волнова́ться | to worry, to be nervous | волну́юсь, волну́ешься
беспоко́иться | to worry, to trouble oneself
зака́зывать / заказа́ть | to order | pf. закажу́, зака́жешь
да́льше | further, on
осторо́жно | careful(ly)
:::

## Making the form

Start from the они́-form of the present tense (or of the perfective future) and remove the ending -ут, -ют, -ат or -ят. What's left is the present stem. Then:

**1. If the stem ends in a vowel, add -й.**

| Infinitive | они́ | Imperative |
|---|---|---|
| чита́ть | [[ns:чита́-ют]] | чита́**й** |
| де́лать | [[ns:де́ла-ют]] | де́ла**й** |
| откры́ть | [[ns:откро́-ют]] | откро́**й** |
| сове́товать | [[ns:сове́ту-ют]] | сове́ту**й** |
| стоя́ть | [[ns:сто-я́т]] | сто**й** |
| петь | [[ns:по-ю́т]] | по**й** |

**2. If the stem ends in a consonant and the я-form is stressed on the ending, add a stressed -и́.**

| Infinitive | они́ | я | Imperative |
|---|---|---|---|
| писа́ть | [[ns:пи́ш-ут]] | пишу́ | пиш**и́** |
| говори́ть | [[ns:говор-я́т]] | говорю́ | говор**и́** |
| сказа́ть | [[ns:ска́ж-ут]] | скажу́ | скаж**и́** |
| смотре́ть | [[ns:смо́тр-ят]] | смотрю́ | смотр**и́** |
| купи́ть | [[ns:ку́п-ят]] | куплю́ | куп**и́** |
| взять | [[ns:возьм-у́т]] | возьму́ | возьм**и́** |
| идти́ | [[ns:ид-у́т]] | иду́ | ид**и́** |
| ждать | [[ns:жд-ут]] | жду | жд**и** |
| помо́чь | [[ns:помо́г-ут]] | помогу́ | помог**и́** |

**3. If the stem ends in a consonant and the я-form is stressed on the stem, add -ь.**

| Infinitive | они́ | я | Imperative |
|---|---|---|---|
| гото́вить | [[ns:гото́в-ят]] | гото́влю | гото́в**ь** |
| отве́тить | [[ns:отве́т-ят]] | отве́чу | отве́т**ь** |
| встать | [[ns:вста́н-ут]] | вста́ну | встан**ь** |
| сесть | [[ns:ся́д-ут]] | ся́ду | сяд**ь** |
| забы́ть | [[ns:забу́д-ут]] | забу́ду | забу́д**ь** |
| быть | [[ns:бу́д-ут]] | бу́ду | буд**ь** |
| пла́кать | [[ns:пла́ч-ут]] | пла́чу | плач**ь** |

One patch on rule 3: if the stem ends in *two* consonants, -ь would be unpronounceable, so the verb takes an unstressed -и instead: по́мнить → по́мни (remember), ко́нчить → ко́нчи (finish), кри́кнуть → кри́кни (shout).

So the shifting-stress verbs you met in the [last section](#s-04-04) behave like end-stressed ones here: пишу́, пи́шешь → пиши́; куплю́, ку́пишь → купи́. It's the я-form that counts.

Notice what happens to consonant mutation. The imperative is built on the они́-stem, so the one-off change in the second-conjugation я-form doesn't appear: куплю́ but купи́, отве́чу but отве́ть, люблю́ but люби́. In the писа́ть family, where the change runs through the whole present tense, it's in the imperative too: пиши́, скажи́, ищи́.

Perfectives with the prefix вы́- keep the stress on the prefix but otherwise copy their base verb: учи́ → вы́учи, иди́ → вы́йди, пей → вы́пей.

### Plural and polite: add -те

For more than one person, or for someone you call вы, add -те to any of the above.

:::ex
чита́й → чита́йте | read
скажи́ → скажи́те | say, tell
гото́вь → гото́вьте | cook, prepare
встань → вста́ньте | stand up
:::

### Reflexive verbs

Verbs in -ся keep their particle: -ся after a consonant or й, -сь after a vowel. (More on these verbs in the [next section](#s-04-06).)

:::ex
сади́ться → сади́сь, сади́тесь | sit down
улыба́ться → улыба́йся, улыба́йтесь | smile
боя́ться → не бо́йся, не бо́йтесь | don't be afraid
:::

### The irregular ones

| Infinitive | ты | вы | |
|---|---|---|---|
| есть | ешь | е́шьте | eat |
| дать | дай | да́йте | give (pf.) |
| дава́ть | дава́й | дава́йте | give (impf.) |
| пить | пей | пе́йте | drink |
| быть | будь | бу́дьте | be |
| лечь | ляг | ля́гте | lie down (pf.) |
| е́хать | поезжа́й | поезжа́йте | go (by vehicle) |

All the -ава́ть verbs keep their -ва- like дава́й does: встава́й (get up), не продава́й (don't sell). Бить, лить and шить follow пить: бей, лей, шей. And е́хать has no imperative of its own, so it borrows поезжа́й.

A few verbs have no imperative in normal use at all, because you can't order someone to do them: ви́деть, слы́шать, хоте́ть, мочь. For "look!" and "listen!" use смотри́ and слу́шай.

## Aspect in positive commands

### Perfective: please do this one thing

When you ask for a single, concrete action and what you want is the result, use the perfective. This is the neutral, default request.

:::ex
**Закро́й** окно́, пожа́луйста. | Close the window, please.
**Скажи́те**, пожа́луйста, где здесь метро́? | Excuse me, could you tell me where the subway is?
**Подожди́** мину́ту. | Wait a minute.
**Позвони́** мне ве́чером. | Call me tonight.
**Переда́й** соль, пожа́луйста. | Pass the salt, please.
**Покажи́те**, пожа́луйста, э́ту су́мку. | Could you show me this bag, please?
**Купи́** хлеб по доро́ге домо́й. | Buy bread on your way home.
:::

### Imperfective: four situations

**1. In general, regularly, always.** Repetition is imperfective, in commands as everywhere else.

:::ex
**Звони́** ча́ще! | Call more often!
Всегда́ **закрыва́й** дверь на ключ. | Always lock the door.
**Пиши́те** нам! | Write to us!
:::

**2. Invitations and offers.** When you invite a guest in, offer them a seat or tell them to help themselves, you use the imperfective. You aren't demanding a result; you're giving a friendly green light to something the person is expected or welcome to do.

:::ex
**Входи́те**! | Come in!
**Проходи́те**, **раздева́йтесь**, **сади́тесь**. | Come through, take your coat off, have a seat.
**Бери́те** ещё, не стесня́йтесь. | Have some more, don't be shy.
**Приходи́те** к нам в суббо́ту. | Come and see us on Saturday.
:::

This set of polite formulas is worth learning by heart. A perfective here (Ся́дьте!) is not rude exactly, but it sounds like an instruction from a doctor or a police officer rather than a host.

**3. "Go on, then."** When the action is already understood, because it's been requested, agreed or is obviously next, the imperfective is the signal to start or to keep going.

:::ex
Ну, **расска́зывай**! | Well, tell me all about it!
**Чита́й** да́льше. | Read on.
**Говори́те**, я слу́шаю. | Go ahead, I'm listening.
**Встава́й**, уже́ де́вять! | Get up, it's nine already!
:::

Compare: Расскажи́, что случи́лось is a normal first request to hear what happened. If the person hesitates, you follow up with Ну, расска́зывай!

**4. The process or the manner.** If the command is about *how* to do something, rather than about getting it done, it's imperfective.

:::ex
**Говори́те**, пожа́луйста, ме́дленнее. | Please speak more slowly.
**Ешь** ме́дленно. | Eat slowly.
**Пиши́** аккура́тно. | Write neatly.
:::

:::warn The impatient imperfective
Because of use 3, an imperfective command for a one-off action that *isn't* an invitation can sound pushy. Закро́й окно́ is a request. Закрыва́й окно́! means "come on, shut the window already". Until you have a feel for it, keep to this division: perfective for specific requests, imperfective for invitations and general rules.
:::

## Aspect in negative commands

### Не + imperfective: don't

To tell someone not to do something, use the imperfective. This is the same pull of negation you saw in [the last aspect section](#s-04-03): you are ruling out the action as such.

:::ex
Не **закрыва́й** окно́, здесь жа́рко. | Don't close the window, it's hot in here.
Не **говори́** ма́ме. | Don't tell Mom.
Не **звони́** мне бо́льше. | Don't call me again.
Не **жди́те** меня́. | Don't wait for me.
Не **волну́йся**. | Don't worry.
Не **беспоко́йтесь**, я сам. | Don't trouble yourself, I'll do it.
:::

So a positive request and its negative counterpart normally use different aspects:

| Do it | Don't do it |
|---|---|
| Купи́ хлеб. | Не покупа́й хлеб. |
| Скажи́ ему́. | Не говори́ ему́. |
| Откро́й дверь. | Не открыва́й дверь. |
| Возьми́ зонт. | Не бери́ зонт. |
| Подожди́ меня́. | Не жди меня́. |

### Не + perfective: watch out

A negated perfective imperative is not a prohibition. It's a **warning** about something the person might do by accident. Nobody intends to fall, forget or be late; you're telling them to take care that it doesn't happen.

:::ex
Не **упади́**! Здесь ско́льзко. | Don't fall! It's slippery here.
Не **забу́дь** ключи́! | Don't forget your keys!
Не **опозда́й**! | Don't be late!
Не **потеря́й** биле́т. | Don't lose your ticket.
:::

Russians often put смотри́ ("mind you", literally "look") in front: Смотри́ не опозда́й! (Mind you're not late!)

The verb "forget" shows both types nicely. Не забу́дь is the warning about one specific thing: Не забу́дь купи́ть молоко́ (Don't forget to buy milk). Не забыва́й is the general instruction: Не забыва́й, что он ещё ребёнок (Don't forget that he's still a child).

## Let's: дава́й

"Let's" is дава́й (to one person you call ты) or дава́йте (to several people, or politely), followed by one of two things.

**Дава́й + the мы-form of a perfective verb**, for a single action:

:::ex
Дава́й **посмо́трим** фильм. | Let's watch a movie.
Дава́й **зака́жем** пи́ццу. | Let's order pizza.
Дава́йте **начнём**. | Let's begin.
Дава́й **поговори́м** за́втра. | Let's talk tomorrow.
:::

**Дава́й + an imperfective infinitive**, for a process or something regular:

:::ex
Дава́й **говори́ть** по-ру́сски. | Let's speak Russian.
Дава́й **смотре́ть** да́льше. | Let's keep watching.
:::

The negative is дава́й не бу́дем + imperfective infinitive:

:::ex
Дава́й не бу́дем об э́том **говори́ть**. | Let's not talk about it.
:::

With verbs of going, the мы-form works on its own, without дава́й: Пойдём! (Let's go!). Even more colloquial are the past-tense forms Пошли́! and Пое́хали!, literally "we've gone", which treat the departure as already settled.

Дава́й has two more everyday uses. With я and a perfective future it offers help, "let me": Дава́й я помогу́ (Let me help), Дава́йте я вам покажу́ (Let me show you). And on its own it's the standard way to agree to a suggestion:

:::dialogue
А: Дава́й зака́жем су́ши. | Let's order sushi.
Б: Дава́й! | Yeah, let's!
:::

## Let him: пусть

For a command aimed at a third person ("let him…", "have her…", "they should…"), put пусть before an ordinary present or perfective-future form.

:::ex
Пусть он **позвони́т** мне за́втра. | Have him call me tomorrow.
Пусть А́ня **ку́пит** хлеб. | Let Anya buy the bread.
Пусть **ждёт**. | Let him wait.
Пусть **де́лают** что хотя́т. | Let them do what they want.
Пусть **бу́дет** так. | So be it.
:::

In casual speech you'll also hear пуска́й, with the same meaning.

## Softening a command

A plain imperative with пожа́луйста is perfectly polite in Russian. It is used far more freely than in English, where we tend to wrap requests in "could you possibly". Скажи́те, пожа́луйста… is how you stop a stranger on the street, and nobody will think you abrupt.

Still, there are softer options:

- **A negative question with the perfective future.** Вы не ска́жете, где метро́? (Could you tell me where the subway is?) Ты не помо́жешь мне? (Could you give me a hand?) The не isn't really negative; it just makes the request less direct.
- **A question with мочь.** Мо́жешь откры́ть окно́? (Can you open the window?) One level up in politeness is Не могли́ бы вы…? (Could you possibly…?), which uses a form explained in [Would and so-that](#s-04-07).
- **The particle -ка**, attached to the imperative, makes a command to a friend or a child more casual and coaxing: Иди́-ка сюда́ (Come here a sec), Дай-ка посмотре́ть (Let me have a look). It's covered with the other particles in [Particles II](#s-07-03).

## The infinitive as a command

On signs, in official instructions and in military-style orders, Russian uses a bare infinitive. It's impersonal and categorical: nobody in particular is being addressed, and no discussion is expected.

:::ex
Не **кури́ть**! | No smoking!
Не **входи́ть**! | No entry!
Рука́ми не **тро́гать**. | Do not touch. | literally "not to touch with hands"
**Молча́ть**! | Silence!
:::

You'll see it in recipes and manuals too. Don't use it on your friends unless you're joking.

| Do it regularly | imperfective | Звони́ ча́ще. |
| Go on, get started | imperfective | Расска́зывай! |
| Don't do it | не + imperfective | Не закрыва́й. |
| Careful, don't (by accident) | не + perfective | Не упади́! |
| Let's (once) | дава́й + pf. мы-form | Дава́й посмо́трим. |
| Let's (process) | дава́й + impf. infinitive | Дава́й смотре́ть. |
