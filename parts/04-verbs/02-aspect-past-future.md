# Aspect in the past and the future

The [previous section](#s-04-01) gave the idea: the perfective packages an action as one complete whole, and the imperfective does everything else. The present is always imperfective, but every past and every future sentence makes you choose.

:::vocab
у́жинать / поу́жинать | to have dinner
обе́дать / пообе́дать | to have lunch
ждать / подожда́ть | to wait (for) | жду, ждёшь; pf. also "wait a bit"
включа́ть / включи́ть | to turn on
приходи́ть / прийти́ | to come, to arrive (on foot)
снима́ть / снять | to take off
про́бовать / попро́бовать | to try, to taste
гуля́ть / погуля́ть | to walk, to stroll, to be out
сиде́ть / посиде́ть | to sit | сижу́, сиди́шь
спать / поспа́ть | to sleep | сплю, спишь
зака́нчивать / зако́нчить | to finish
статья́ | article | f.
заря́дка | charger | f.; colloquial
ку́ртка | jacket | f.
за + acc. | in (the time it takes to finish)
:::

## The past

### The event and the background

An imperfective past paints a scene that was in progress, and a perfective past drops an event into it.

:::ex
Когда́ ты **позвони́л**, я **у́жинал**. | When you called, I was having dinner.
Я **чита́л** статью́, а Ма́ша **смотре́ла** сериа́л. | I was reading an article and Masha was watching a series.
Мы **сиде́ли** на ку́хне и **говори́ли**. Вдруг кто-то **позвони́л** в дверь. | We were sitting in the kitchen talking. Suddenly someone rang the doorbell.
:::

Позвони́л is a point; у́жинал is a stretch of time that the point lands in.

### Sequence and simultaneity

A string of **perfectives** is read as a sequence: each event is completed before the next one starts.

:::ex
Она́ **пришла́** домо́й, **сняла́** ку́ртку и **включи́ла** телеви́зор. | She came home, took off her jacket and turned on the TV.
:::

A string of **imperfectives** is read as things going on at the same time, in no particular order.

:::ex
Мы **сиде́ли** на ку́хне, **пи́ли** чай и **говори́ли** о жи́зни. | We sat in the kitchen, drank tea and talked about life.
:::

The same logic changes what когда́ (when) means:

:::ex
Когда́ я **гото́вил** у́жин, пришла́ Ма́ша. | While I was making dinner, Masha arrived.
Когда́ я **пригото́вил** у́жин, пришла́ Ма́ша. | When I had made dinner, Masha arrived.
:::

English needs "while" and "had made" to show the difference. Russian changes nothing but the aspect.

### How long? versus how quickly?

Saying how long something *went on* looks at the process, so the verb is imperfective. The length of time stands in the accusative with no preposition.

:::ex
Я **чита́л** два часа́. | I read for two hours.
Мы **жда́ли** авто́бус со́рок мину́т. | We waited forty minutes for the bus.
Она́ весь ве́чер **писа́ла** отчёт. | She spent the whole evening writing the report.
:::

Saying how long it took to *get something done* is about the finished whole, so the verb is perfective, and the time takes the preposition за.

:::ex
Я **прочита́л** статью́ за два часа́. | I read the article in two hours.
Она́ **вы́учила** все слова́ за оди́н ве́чер. | She learned all the words in one evening.
Он **написа́л** отчёт за час. | He wrote the report in an hour.
:::

Чита́л два часа́ is time spent; прочита́л за два часа́ is a job done in that time. The two patterns don't mix: two hours with за wants a perfective, two hours without it wants an imperfective.

### Repetition

Something done many times is not one event, so repeated and habitual actions are imperfective, however completely each one was carried out. The usual signal words:

:::vocab
всегда́ | always
ча́сто | often
обы́чно | usually
иногда́ | sometimes
ре́дко | rarely
ка́ждый день | every day
по суббо́там | on Saturdays | по + dative plural
ра́ньше | before, in the past, used to
:::

:::ex
В шко́ле я всегда́ **встава́л** в семь. | At school I always got up at seven.
Сего́дня я **встал** в де́сять. | Today I got up at ten.
По суббо́там мы **у́жинали** у ба́бушки. | On Saturdays we used to have dinner at Grandma's.
В суббо́ту мы **поу́жинали** у ба́бушки. | On Saturday we had dinner at Grandma's.
Ра́ньше он ча́сто **звони́л**. | He used to call often.
:::

English "used to" and "would" (in the sense of "we would have dinner at Grandma's") are almost always a Russian imperfective past.

:::note Counted repetitions
When you give an exact number of times, the imperfective is still the normal choice: Я звони́л тебе́ три ра́за! (I called you three times!). But if the speaker bundles the repetitions into a single step of a story, the perfective is possible: Он два ра́за прочита́л сообще́ние и ничего́ не по́нял (He read the message twice and understood nothing). When in doubt, use the imperfective.
:::

:::note Быва́ть: "to be" on repeat
The verb быть has a relative made specially for repeated or occasional being: **быва́ть** (быва́ю, быва́ешь). It means "to be somewhere from time to time", "to visit", or, of events, "to happen now and then", and you will hear it constantly.

- Я ча́сто быва́ю в Москве́. — I'm often in Moscow.
- Он ре́дко быва́ет до́ма. — He's rarely at home.
- Ты быва́л в Ита́лии? — Have you ever been to Italy?
- Так быва́ет. — It happens. / That's how it goes.
- Тако́го не быва́ет! — Things like that don't happen!
:::

### The bare fact: did it ever happen?

This use surprises English speakers most. When you only want to establish whether an action took place at all, with no story around it and no interest in the result, Russian uses the imperfective, even though the action was completed.

:::ex
Ты **чита́л** «Войну́ и мир»? | Have you read "War and Peace"?
Я уже́ **смотре́л** э́тот фильм. | I've already seen this movie.
Я где-то **ви́дел** э́того челове́ка. | I've seen this man somewhere.
Ты [[ns:когда́-нибудь]] **про́бовал** квас? | Have you ever tried kvass?
Я тебе́ **говори́л**! | I told you!
Борщ о́чень вку́сный. Кто **гото́вил**? | The borscht is delicious. Who made it?
:::

The perfective version of the first question exists, but it asks something different:

:::dialogue
А: Ты чита́л «Войну́ и мир»? | Have you (ever) read "War and Peace"?
Б: Чита́л, ещё в шко́ле. | I have, back in school.
:::

:::dialogue
А: Ты прочита́л «Войну́ и мир»? | Did you finish "War and Peace"?
Б: Ещё нет. Там ты́сяча страни́ц! | Not yet. It's a thousand pages!
:::

Чита́л? asks about your life experience. Прочита́л? assumes we both know you were supposed to read it, or were in the middle of it, and asks whether that job is done. The perfective always has some concrete, expected result in mind.

So the everyday "have you eaten?" questions go both ways. Ты обе́дал? is a neutral inquiry; Ты пообе́дал? sounds more like "have you had your lunch (so we can go)?"

### Result still standing, or undone?

A perfective past says the result was reached and, unless you hear otherwise, still holds. An imperfective past makes no such promise. So for actions that can be reversed, such as opening, coming, taking or turning on, the imperfective often implies that the action happened *and has since been undone*.

:::ex
Кто **откры́л** окно́? | Who opened the window? | it is open now
Кто **открыва́л** окно́? | Who had the window open? | it is closed again now, but the room is cold
Ко мне **пришёл** друг. | A friend has come to see me. | he's here
Ко мне **приходи́л** друг. | A friend came by. | and has left again
Я **взял** твою́ заря́дку. | I've taken your charger. | I have it
Я **брал** твою́ заря́дку. | I borrowed your charger. | and it's back in its place
Ты **включа́л** мой компью́тер? | Did you have my computer on? | it's off now, but something has changed
:::

Textbooks call this the "annulled" action: the perfective points at a result that is in force, and the imperfective is what you use for a round trip. This matters most with verbs of coming and going; see [Going](#s-05-02).

### "For a while": the prefix по-

Process verbs like гуля́ть (to stroll), сиде́ть (to sit), спать (to sleep), рабо́тать (to work), говори́ть (to talk) have no natural finishing line. To put such an activity into a chain of events, Russian uses the prefix **по-**: it makes a perfective meaning "do it for a while (and stop)".

| Imperfective | "For a while" perfective |
|---|---|
| чита́ть | почита́ть — read for a bit |
| гуля́ть | погуля́ть — go for a walk |
| сиде́ть | посиде́ть — sit for a while |
| спать | поспа́ть — get some sleep |
| рабо́тать | порабо́тать — do some work |
| говори́ть | поговори́ть — have a talk |
| ду́мать | поду́мать — think (it over) |
| ждать | подожда́ть — wait a bit |

:::ex
Мы **посиде́ли** в кафе́, а пото́м **погуля́ли** по па́рку. | We sat in a café for a while and then took a walk in the park.
Я немно́го **почита́л** и лёг спать. | I read a little and went to bed.
Мне на́до **поду́мать**. | I need to think about it.
Я **поспа́л** два часа́ и опя́ть сел рабо́тать. | I slept for two hours and sat down to work again.
:::

These verbs are perfective because the action is bounded, by a stretch of time rather than a result. Compare Я чита́л (I was reading: a scene) with Я почита́л (I did a bit of reading: a step in the story) and Я прочита́л кни́гу (I read the book through: a result).

So по- has two jobs. With some verbs it is the plain perfective partner (позвони́ть, посмотре́ть, поу́жинать); with open-ended activities it means "for a while". Often the two blur: посмотре́ть фильм is "watch the movie", посмотре́ть телеви́зор is "watch some TV".

## The future

### Two futures

The imperfective future is бу́ду + imperfective infinitive. The perfective future is a single word, the perfective verb with ordinary present-tense endings.

| | Imperfective future | Perfective future |
|---|---|---|
| я | бу́ду де́лать | сде́лаю |
| ты | бу́дешь де́лать | сде́лаешь |
| он, она́ | бу́дет де́лать | сде́лает |
| мы | бу́дем де́лать | сде́лаем |
| вы | бу́дете де́лать | сде́лаете |
| они́ | бу́дут де́лать | сде́лают |

Never бу́ду with a perfective infinitive.

Perfective verbs conjugate like any other verb, quirks included. These eight you will use daily. Watch the stress: in several of them it jumps back after the я-form.

| | сде́лать (do) | написа́ть (write) | сказа́ть (say) | купи́ть (buy) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| я | сде́лаю | напишу́ | скажу́ | куплю́ |
| ты | сде́лаешь | напи́шешь | ска́жешь | ку́пишь |
| он, она́ | сде́лает | напи́шет | ска́жет | ку́пит |
| мы | сде́лаем | напи́шем | ска́жем | ку́пим |
| вы | сде́лаете | напи́шете | ска́жете | ку́пите |
| они́ | сде́лают | напи́шут | ска́жут | ку́пят |

| | дать (give) | взять (take) | поня́ть (understand) | смочь (be able) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| я | дам | возьму́ | пойму́ | смогу́ |
| ты | дашь | возьмёшь | поймёшь | смо́жешь |
| он, она́ | даст | возьмёт | поймёт | смо́жет |
| мы | дади́м | возьмём | поймём | смо́жем |
| вы | дади́те | возьмёте | поймёте | смо́жете |
| они́ | даду́т | возьму́т | пойму́т | смо́гут |

Дать is one of the few truly irregular verbs in the language. The others are explained in [Conjugation patterns in depth](#s-04-04).

### When to use which

The logic is the same as in the past.

**Perfective future: one action, carried through to its result.** Promises, plans, offers, predictions of a single event.

:::ex
Я **позвоню́** тебе́ ве́чером. | I'll call you tonight.
Сейча́с **сде́лаю**. | I'll do it right now.
Я **куплю́** хлеб по доро́ге домо́й. | I'll buy bread on the way home.
Он не **поймёт**. | He won't understand.
Ты **смо́жешь** прийти́ в пя́тницу? | Will you be able to come on Friday?
:::

**Imperfective future: process, duration, repetition, or simply "what I'll be busy with".**

:::ex
За́втра я весь день **бу́ду рабо́тать**. | Tomorrow I'll be working all day.
Я **бу́ду звони́ть** тебе́ ка́ждый день. | I'll call you every day.
Что ты **бу́дешь де́лать** в суббо́ту? | What will you be doing on Saturday?
Ве́чером я **бу́ду смотре́ть** футбо́л. | Tonight I'm going to watch soccer.
:::

Side by side:

:::ex
Я **бу́ду писа́ть** отчёт весь ве́чер. | I'll be writing the report all evening.
Я **напишу́** отчёт за ве́чер. | I'll get the report written in an evening.
Я **бу́ду ждать** тебя́ у вхо́да. | I'll be waiting for you at the entrance.
Я **подожду́**. | I'll wait (a bit). / I can wait.
:::

In conversation the perfective future is the more frequent of the two, because most of what we say about the future is promises and single planned results. Бу́ду де́лать puts the activity itself in the spotlight.

A chain of perfective futures is a sequence, just as in the past:

:::ex
Я **зако́нчу** рабо́ту, **куплю́** проду́кты и **пригото́влю** у́жин. | I'll finish work, buy groceries and make dinner.
:::

:::note Бу́дешь?
The imperfective future is also how you ask about someone's intentions, especially with food and drink: Ты бу́дешь есть? (Are you going to eat?), Вы бу́дете зака́зывать? (Are you going to order?). In casual speech the infinitive drops out altogether: Ко́фе бу́дешь? — Бу́ду. (Want some coffee? — Yes, please.)
:::

### "When I finish": future means future

English uses a present tense after "when" and "if" even when the time is future: "when I finish, I'll write". Russian puts the verb in the future in both halves of the sentence.

:::ex
Когда́ я **зако́нчу**, я тебе́ **напишу́**. | When I finish, I'll text you.
Я **скажу́** ему́, когда́ **уви́жу**. | I'll tell him when I see him.
Е́сли **бу́дет** вре́мя, я **позвоню́**. | If there's time, I'll call.
:::

### The present as a future

As in English, a present-tense imperfective can stand for a fixed plan, as long as a time word makes the future clear.

:::ex
За́втра я **рабо́таю**. | I'm working tomorrow.
В пя́тницу мы **у́жинаем** у О́ли. | We're having dinner at Olya's on Friday.
:::

This works only with the imperfective present. A perfective "present" form *is* the future already.

:::aside When the perfective future isn't about the future
Now and then you'll hear a negated perfective future describing a present difficulty: Не пойму́, что он говори́т (I can't make out what he's saying), Ника́к не найду́ ключи́ (I just can't find my keys). The idea is "however I try, the result doesn't come". Recognize it; you don't have to use it.
:::

| Meaning | Past | Future |
|---|---|---|
| One completed event; result in force | прочита́л | прочита́ю |
| Process, background, "was doing" | чита́л | бу́ду чита́ть |
| Duration (два часа́) | чита́л | бу́ду чита́ть |
| Done within a time (за два часа́) | прочита́л | прочита́ю |
| Repeated, habitual | чита́л | бу́ду чита́ть |
| Bare fact: "ever?" | чита́л | — |
| Reversed, round-trip action | открыва́л, приходи́л | — |
| For a while, then on to the next thing | почита́л | почита́ю |
:::
