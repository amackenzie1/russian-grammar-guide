# Aspect with infinitives and negation

Infinitives come in pairs too: де́лать and сде́лать are both "to do". So every time you say "I want to…", "I need to…", "I started to…" or "you can't…", you have to pick one. The principle is the same: one whole action with a result → perfective; process, repetition, or the action "in general" → imperfective.

In many of these constructions the word before the infinitive forces one aspect. And negation pushes hard toward the imperfective.

:::vocab
начина́ть / нача́ть | to begin
продолжа́ть / продо́лжить | to continue
конча́ть / ко́нчить | to finish, to stop
перестава́ть / переста́ть | to stop, to cease
броса́ть / бро́сить | to quit; to throw
привыка́ть / привы́кнуть | to get used to | past привы́к, привы́кла
надоеда́ть / надое́сть | to bore, to get tiresome | мне надое́ло = I'm sick of
успева́ть / успе́ть | to manage (in time)
удава́ться / уда́ться | to succeed | мне удало́сь = I managed to
кури́ть | to smoke | курю́, ку́ришь
входи́ть / войти́ | to enter, to go in
води́ть маши́ну | to drive (a car)
ра́но | early
бо́льше | more; (with не) any more
:::

## Starting, continuing, stopping: always imperfective

After a verb that means *begin*, *continue* or *stop*, the infinitive is **always imperfective**. No exceptions. You can only start, continue or stop a process; an action seen as a finished whole has no inside to start or stop in.

:::ex
Я на́чал **учи́ть** ру́сский год наза́д. | I started learning Russian a year ago. | год наза́д = a year ago
Она́ начала́ **рабо́тать** в де́вять. | She started working at nine.
Мы продолжа́ем **иска́ть** кварти́ру. | We're still looking for an apartment. | literally "we continue to look"
Он переста́л мне **писа́ть**. | He stopped writing to me.
Па́па бро́сил **кури́ть**. | Dad quit smoking.
Я зако́нчил **рабо́тать** в шесть. | I finished working at six.
:::

For "finish", everyday speech prefers зака́нчивать / зако́нчить to the shorter конча́ть / ко́нчить; both follow the same rule.

The *first* verb can be either aspect (начина́ю, на́чал, начну́); only the infinitive after it is fixed. Note the stress in the past of нача́ть: на́чал, начала́, на́чали.

The verb **стать**, which you know as "to become", also means "to start" when an infinitive follows. It's very common in storytelling, and its negative не стал means "didn't (bother to)", "decided not to".

:::ex
По́сле шко́лы он стал **рабо́тать** в ба́нке. | After school he started working at a bank.
Я не ста́ла **ждать** и ушла́. | I didn't bother waiting and left.
Я не стал ему́ **звони́ть**. | I decided not to call him.
:::

## Habits, skills, likes: imperfective

A second group of words normally takes the imperfective infinitive too. They describe your relationship to an activity *in general*, not to one occasion of it:

- привыка́ть / привы́кнуть — to get used to
- уме́ть — to know how to
- учи́ться / научи́ться — to learn how to
- люби́ть — to love, to like doing
- нра́виться — to like, to enjoy
- надоеда́ть / надое́сть — to get sick of
- устава́ть / уста́ть — to get tired of

:::ex
Я привы́к **встава́ть** ра́но. | I'm used to getting up early.
Она́ уме́ет **гото́вить**. | She can cook.
Я учу́сь **води́ть** маши́ну. | I'm learning to drive.
Я люблю́ **гото́вить**, но не люблю́ **мыть** посу́ду. | I love cooking but I don't like washing the dishes.
Мне нра́вится **гуля́ть** по го́роду. | I like walking around the city.
Мне надое́ло **ждать**. | I'm sick of waiting.
Я уста́л **объясня́ть**. | I'm tired of explaining.
:::

## Managing and forgetting: perfective

A small group goes the other way. These verbs are about whether one particular action *got done*, so the infinitive is perfective:

- успе́ть — to manage in time
- забы́ть — to forget
- уда́ться — to succeed (used impersonally with the dative: мне удало́сь)

:::ex
Я не успе́л **поза́втракать**. | I didn't have time for breakfast.
Мы успе́ли **купи́ть** биле́ты. | We managed to get tickets in time.
Я забы́л **купи́ть** хлеб. | I forgot to buy bread.
Ты забы́ла **закры́ть** дверь. | You forgot to close the door.
Мне удало́сь **найти́** хоро́шую кварти́ру. | I managed to find a good apartment.
:::

This is a rule for the perfective forms успе́ть, забы́ть and уда́ться, which are what you'll need nine times out of ten.

## Wanting, needing, being able: you choose

After хоте́ть (to want), мочь (to be able), на́до and ну́жно (need to), до́лжен (must), and verbs like реши́ть (to decide), обеща́ть (to promise), проси́ть (to ask), both aspects are possible, and the ordinary meanings apply.

**One specific action with a result: perfective.** This is the more common case in everyday talk, because we mostly want and need particular things to get done.

:::ex
Я хочу́ **посмотре́ть** э́тот фильм. | I want to see this movie.
Мне на́до **позвони́ть** ма́ме. | I need to call Mom.
Мо́жешь **откры́ть** окно́? | Can you open the window?
Я хочу́ вам **сказа́ть** одну́ вещь. | I want to tell you one thing.
Он обеща́л **помо́чь**. | He promised to help.
:::

**The activity as such, a process, or something repeated: imperfective.**

:::ex
Я хочу́ **смотре́ть** фи́льмы без субти́тров. | I want to watch movies without subtitles. | as a general ability
Мне на́до бо́льше **спать**. | I need to sleep more.
На́до **звони́ть** ма́ме ча́ще. | I should call Mom more often.
Я хочу́ **жить** у мо́ря. | I want to live by the sea.
Хочу́ **есть**. | I'm hungry. | literally "I want to eat"
:::

Compare:

:::ex
Я хочу́ **купи́ть** маши́ну. | I want to buy a car. | one purchase
Я хочу́ **покупа́ть** проду́кты на ры́нке. | I want to buy my groceries at the market. | as a habit
Вам на́до **прочита́ть** э́ту статью́. | You need to read this article. | this one, all of it
Вам на́до бо́льше **чита́ть**. | You need to read more. | in general
:::

:::note "Time to get going"
With на́до, ну́жно and пора́ (it's time), the imperfective infinitive has one more job: it says that it's time to *get started* on something, especially something already expected. Уже́ по́здно, на́до идти́ (It's late, I should get going). Семь часо́в, пора́ встава́ть (It's seven o'clock, time to get up). На́до встать would be about the result: "I must (manage to) get up".
:::

## Negation pushes toward the imperfective

Take a sentence with a perfective infinitive, make it negative, and very often the infinitive flips to imperfective:

:::ex
На́до **купи́ть** молоко́. | We need to buy milk.
Не на́до **покупа́ть** молоко́, у нас есть. | No need to buy milk, we have some.
Я хочу́ ему́ **сказа́ть**. | I want to tell him.
Я не хочу́ ему́ **говори́ть**. | I don't want to tell him.
Сове́тую **посмотре́ть** э́тот фильм. | I recommend seeing this movie.
Не сове́тую **смотре́ть** э́тот фильм. | I don't recommend seeing this movie.
:::

A perfective infinitive aims at a result. When you say an action is unnecessary, unwanted or inadvisable, there is no result to aim at; you're rejecting the action as such, and that is imperfective territory.

In these patterns it is a firm rule:

**не на́до, не ну́жно, не сто́ит (it's not worth) + imperfective**

:::ex
Не на́до ему́ **звони́ть**. | Don't call him. / There's no need to call him.
Не ну́жно ничего́ **объясня́ть**. | There's no need to explain anything.
Не сто́ит **покупа́ть** э́тот телефо́н. | It's not worth buying this phone.
:::

**Deciding, promising, asking not to do something: не + imperfective**

:::ex
Мы реши́ли не **покупа́ть** маши́ну. | We decided not to buy a car.
Он обеща́л не **опа́здывать**. | He promised not to be late.
Она́ попроси́ла не **звони́ть** по́сле десяти́. | She asked us not to call after ten.
:::

**не хочу́ + imperfective** is the default:

:::ex
Я не хочу́ **говори́ть** об э́том. | I don't want to talk about it.
Не хочу́ **встава́ть**. | I don't want to get up.
Я не хочу́ ничего́ **реша́ть** сего́дня. | I don't want to decide anything today.
:::

There is one exception. If what you don't want is an *accidental* or unwelcome outcome, the perfective comes back, because now the result is exactly what you have in mind:

:::ex
Я не хочу́ **опозда́ть**. | I don't want to be late.
Я не хочу́ тебя́ **оби́деть**. | I don't want to hurt your feelings.
:::

### Нельзя́: forbidden or impossible

Нельзя́ covers both "you may not" and "you cannot", and the aspect of the infinitive tells you which.

- **нельзя́ + imperfective** = it's forbidden, you shouldn't
- **нельзя́ + perfective** = it's impossible, it can't be done

:::ex
Сюда́ нельзя́ **входи́ть**. | You're not allowed in here.
В ко́мнату нельзя́ **войти́**: нет ключа́. | You can't get into the room: there's no key.
Э́то окно́ нельзя́ **открыва́ть**. | This window mustn't be opened.
Э́то окно́ нельзя́ **откры́ть**. | This window can't be opened. | it's stuck
Э́то нельзя́ **забыва́ть**. | We must not forget this.
Э́то нельзя́ **забы́ть**. | This is impossible to forget.
:::

A ban applies to the action in general; "impossible" means the result cannot be reached.

### Не могу́

After не мочь both aspects occur. The perfective says you can't achieve this one result; the imperfective says the whole activity is out of the question.

:::ex
Не могу́ **откры́ть** дверь. | I can't get the door open.
Не могу́ **найти́** телефо́н. | I can't find my phone.
Я бо́льше не могу́ **ждать**. | I can't wait any longer.
Извини́, не могу́ сейча́с **говори́ть**. | Sorry, I can't talk right now.
:::

:::note Where не stands matters
Put не *after* мочь and the meaning changes again, with aspect once more doing the work:

Он мо́жет не прийти́. — He might not come. (perfective: a possible outcome)
Он мо́жет не приходи́ть. — He doesn't have to come. (imperfective: the action isn't needed)

Likewise мо́жно не + imperfective means "you don't have to": Мо́жно не отвеча́ть (You don't have to answer).
:::

## Не чита́л and не прочита́л

Negation matters for ordinary past-tense verbs too.

**Не + imperfective past** is the plain, neutral way to say that something didn't happen.

**Не + perfective past** says that an *expected* result failed to appear. Someone was supposed to do it, or started doing it, or you were waiting for it, and it didn't come off.

:::ex
Я не **чита́л** э́ту кни́гу. | I haven't read this book. | never opened it
Я не **прочита́л** кни́гу. | I didn't finish the book. | I was meant to, or I started
Он не **звони́л**. | He hasn't called. | there has been no call
Он не **позвони́л**. | He didn't call. | he said he would, I was waiting
Я не **сдава́л** экза́мен. | I didn't take the exam.
Я не **сдал** экза́мен. | I failed the exam.
:::

Не сдава́л: I wasn't even there. Не сдал: I was there, and it went badly.

The imperfective is also the aspect of flat denial. Accused of something, you deny that the action occurred at all:

:::dialogue
А: Кто взял мои́ ключи́? | Who took my keys?
Б: Я не брал! | I didn't! | literally "I wasn't taking": no such action on my part
:::

And it's what you use with давно́ (for a long time) and ещё (yet) when nothing has happened:

:::ex
Я давно́ не **ви́дел** Са́шу. | I haven't seen Sasha in a long time.
Я ещё не **обе́дал**. | I haven't had lunch yet.
:::

With ещё не, the perfective is equally common when a result is clearly expected: Я ещё не реши́л (I haven't decided yet), Она́ ещё не пришла́ (She hasn't arrived yet).

## Не бу́ду and не сде́лаю

The future splits the same way. **Не бу́ду + imperfective** is the standard way to say "I'm not going to", including a flat refusal.

:::ex
Я не бу́ду э́то **есть**. | I'm not eating that.
Я не бу́ду ему́ **звони́ть**. | I'm not going to call him.
Мы не бу́дем **ждать**. | We won't wait.
:::

**Не + perfective future** predicts that a particular result won't come about. Often it means "won't manage to", and sometimes it's a promise that one specific thing won't happen.

:::ex
Он не **поймёт**. | He won't understand.
Мы не **успе́ем**. | We won't make it.
Я никому́ не **скажу́**. | I won't tell anyone.
:::

Compare Я не бу́ду э́то есть (I refuse) with Я сто́лько не съем (I won't be able to eat that much).

:::summary
| Before the infinitive | Aspect of the infinitive |
|---|---|
| нача́ть, стать, продолжа́ть, ко́нчить, переста́ть, бро́сить | imperfective only |
| привы́кнуть, уме́ть, учи́ться, люби́ть, нра́виться, надое́сть, уста́ть | imperfective |
| успе́ть, забы́ть, уда́ться | perfective |
| хочу́, могу́, на́до, ну́жно, до́лжен | either: one result → pf.; process, habit → impf. |
| не на́до, не ну́жно, не сто́ит, не сове́тую | imperfective |
| не хочу́ | imperfective (pf. for an unwelcome accident) |
| нельзя́ | impf. = forbidden; pf. = impossible |

In the past: не чита́л = it didn't happen; не прочита́л = the expected result didn't come.
:::
