# Would and so-that: бы and что́бы

So far every sentence has been about reality: what happens, happened or will happen. This section is about everything else. What *would* happen, what you'd *like* to happen, what you want *someone else* to do, and what you do something *in order to* achieve.

English handles all this with a heap of machinery: would, would have, could, should, were, had, "I want him to", "so that". Russian uses one tiny word, **бы**, plus a form you already know inside out. It is one of the rare places where Russian grammar is simpler than English.

:::vocab
бы | would | particle; never stressed
е́сли бы | if (unreal)
что́бы | so that, in order to; that (after wanting, telling)
на твоём ме́сте | in your place, if I were you
соглаша́ться / согласи́ться | to agree
отдыха́ть / отдохну́ть | to rest
проси́ть / попроси́ть | to ask (for something), to request | прошу́, про́сишь
запи́сывать / записа́ть | to write down
буди́ть / разбуди́ть | to wake (someone) up
шуме́ть | to make noise | шумлю́, шуми́шь
ва́жно | (it is) important
гла́вное | the main thing
во́время | on time
здо́рово | great, awesome
:::

## One form for all of "would"

To say that something is hypothetical rather than real, take the **past-tense form** of the verb (the one in -л) and add **бы**.

:::ex
Я пошёл. | I went.
Я **бы пошёл**. | I would go. / I would have gone.
Она́ согласи́лась. | She agreed.
Она́ **бы согласи́лась**. | She would agree. / She would have agreed.
:::

Two things to take in.

First, the -л form here is *not past*. Together with бы it carries no time at all. Я бы пошёл can be about yesterday, now or next week, and only the context tells you which:

:::ex
Я **бы пошёл** за́втра, но я рабо́таю. | I would go tomorrow, but I'm working.
Я **бы пошёл** вчера́, но я рабо́тал. | I would have gone yesterday, but I was working.
:::

There is no separate "would have". English speakers keep looking for it; it isn't there.

Second, the verb agrees the way a past-tense verb does: by gender and number, not by person. A man says я бы хоте́л, a woman я бы хоте́ла, and several people мы бы хоте́ли.

:::ex
Я **бы купи́л** э́ту маши́ну, но она́ сли́шком дорога́я. | I'd buy this car, but it's too expensive.
Что **бы** ты **сде́лал**? | What would you do? / What would you have done?
Э́то **бы́ло бы** здо́рово! | That would be great!
Я **бы** так не **сказа́л**. | I wouldn't say that.
:::

Aspect works as usual: perfective for one complete action (я бы купи́л), imperfective for a process or habit (я бы ка́ждый день гуля́л, "I'd go for a walk every day").

## Where бы goes

Бы has no stress of its own, so it can't start a sentence; it leans on the word before it. It has two favorite positions:

- right after the first stressed word or phrase of the clause (most common in speech);
- right after the verb.

:::ex
Я **бы** хоте́л ко́фе. | I'd like a coffee.
Я хоте́л **бы** ко́фе. | I'd like a coffee.
На твоём ме́сте я **бы** подожда́л. | If I were you, I'd wait.
Мы с удово́льствием пришли́ **бы**. | We'd be glad to come.
:::

There's no real difference in meaning. One бы per clause is enough. After a word ending in a vowel it's often shortened to б in casual speech: Я б не пошёл.

## If only: е́сли бы

An unreal condition ("if I had money", when I don't) is е́сли бы + past form. The result clause has its own бы + past form. Both halves get a бы.

:::ex
**Е́сли бы** у меня́ бы́ли де́ньги, я **бы купи́л** маши́ну. | If I had money, I'd buy a car. / If I'd had money, I'd have bought a car.
**Е́сли бы** ты позвони́л, я **бы пришла́**. | If you had called, I would have come.
**Е́сли бы** я знал, я **бы** тебе́ **сказа́л**. | If I knew, I'd tell you. / If I had known, I'd have told you.
Что **бы** ты де́лал, **е́сли бы** у тебя́ был миллио́н? | What would you do if you had a million?
:::

You can put то ("then") at the start of the second half; it adds a little weight and nothing else: Е́сли бы я знал, то я бы сказа́л.

Compare a real condition, about something that may well happen. That's plain е́сли with ordinary tenses and no бы:

:::ex
Е́сли **бу́дет** вре́мя, я **приду́**. | If there's time, I'll come. | quite possible
Е́сли бы **бы́ло** вре́мя, я бы **пришёл**. | If there were time, I'd come. | but there isn't
:::

Two handy fixed patterns. **Е́сли бы не** + nominative means "if it weren't for":

:::ex
Е́сли бы не ты, я бы опозда́л. | If it weren't for you, I'd have been late.
Е́сли бы не дождь, мы бы пошли́ гуля́ть. | If it weren't for the rain, we'd go for a walk.
:::

And an е́сли бы clause on its own is a sigh of regret:

:::ex
Е́сли бы я знал! | If only I'd known!
:::

## Wishes, cravings and gentle advice

Because бы turns a statement into "this isn't real, I'm only imagining it", it's the natural way to soften things.

**A polite wish.** Я хочу́ is direct: "I want". Я бы хоте́л is "I would like".

:::ex
Я **бы хоте́л** поговори́ть с ва́ми. | I'd like to speak with you.
Я **хоте́ла бы** заказа́ть сто́лик. | I'd like to book a table.
:::

**A craving.** With any verb, я бы + past form says what you're in the mood for.

:::ex
Я **бы вы́пил** ко́фе. | I could go for a coffee.
Я **бы** сейча́с **поспа́ла**. | I'd love a nap right now.
:::

**Soft advice.** Ты бы + past form suggests rather than tells: "you might…", "why don't you…". Depending on the tone, it can also be a mild reproach.

:::ex
Ты **бы отдохну́л**. | You should get some rest.
Ты **бы позвони́ла** ма́ме. | You might give your mom a call.
Вы **бы се́ли**. | Why don't you sit down?
:::

**Wishful thinking.** Вот бы… ("if only…", "wouldn't it be nice…") can be followed by a past form, an infinitive, or just a phrase with no verb at all.

:::ex
**Вот бы** сейча́с на мо́ре! | Oh, to be at the seaside right now!
**Вот бы** вы́учить ру́сский за ме́сяц! | If only you could learn Russian in a month!
**Вот бы** он пришёл! | If only he'd come!
:::

**The one thing that matters.** Лишь бы… means "as long as…", "I just hope…": everything else is negotiable.

:::ex
**Лишь бы** [[ns:не́ было]] дождя́. | As long as it doesn't rain.
**Лишь бы** успе́ть. | I just hope we make it.
**Лишь бы** она́ согласи́лась. | As long as she agrees.
:::

## Polite requests

Put мочь into the бы form and you get "could you…?". A не is usually added; as with the negative requests in [the imperative section](#s-04-05), it only makes the question less pushy.

:::ex
**Не могли́ бы вы** мне помо́чь? | Could you possibly help me?
**Вы не могли́ бы** говори́ть ме́дленнее? | Could you speak more slowly?
**Ты не мог бы** закры́ть окно́? | Could you close the window?
:::

This is the politest everyday request formula in Russian, suitable for strangers, officials and bosses. With friends it can sound a little stiff, or pointed; a simple Закро́й окно́, пожа́луйста is warmer.

Two more useful softeners in the same vein:

:::ex
Я **хоте́л бы** спроси́ть… | I wanted to ask… / I'd like to ask…
**Бы́ло бы** хорошо́ встре́титься за́втра. | It would be good to meet tomorrow.
:::

## Что́бы: что + бы

Now the second word of the title. **Что́бы** is simply что and бы fused together, and the бы inside it still does its job: the clause after что́бы describes something not real, only wanted or intended. That explains the rule that looks so strange at first:

**After что́бы with its own subject, the verb is in the past-tense form**, whatever the actual time. The бы is already there, inside что́бы, so you never add another one.

In speech что́бы is unstressed, and you'll often hear and see the short version чтоб.

### Wanting someone else to do something

English says "I want you to come". Russian can't build it that way; there is no Я хочу́ тебя́ прийти́. You have to say "I want that you came":

:::ex
Я хочу́, **что́бы ты пришёл**. | I want you to come.
Она́ хо́чет, **что́бы я позвони́л**. | She wants me to call.
Ма́ма хо́чет, **что́бы мы бы́ли** до́ма в де́сять. | Mom wants us to be home at ten.
Ты хо́чешь, **что́бы я помо́г**? | Do you want me to help?
Я не хочу́, **что́бы ты уходи́л**. | I don't want you to leave.
:::

When the person wanting and the person doing are the same, there's nothing to it: just хоте́ть + infinitive, as always.

:::ex
Я хочу́ **прийти́**. | I want to come.
Я хочу́, **что́бы ты пришёл**. | I want you to come.
:::

### Telling, asking, and things that need to happen

The same construction follows verbs of telling and asking when what's being passed on is an instruction, not information.

:::ex
Он сказа́л, **что́бы я подожда́л**. | He told me to wait.
Скажи́ Ди́ме, **что́бы он купи́л** хлеб. | Tell Dima to buy bread.
Она́ попроси́ла, **что́бы мы не шуме́ли**. | She asked us not to make noise.
Напиши́ О́ле, **что́бы она́** нас не **ждала́**. | Text Olya not to wait for us.
:::

Here the choice between что and что́бы changes the meaning completely:

:::ex
Он сказа́л, **что** подождёт. | He said that he would wait. | information
Он сказа́л, **что́бы** я подожда́л. | He told me to wait. | instruction
:::

(With проси́ть you can also use a plain infinitive: Она́ попроси́ла меня́ подожда́ть. There's more on что versus что́бы in [That, whether, and reported speech](#s-06-02).)

Что́бы also follows words that say something is necessary, important or desirable:

:::ex
На́до, **что́бы** все **пришли́** во́время. | Everyone needs to come on time.
Ва́жно, **что́бы** ты э́то **понима́л**. | It's important that you understand this.
Гла́вное, **что́бы** тебе́ **нра́вилось**. | The main thing is that you like it.
:::

### Purpose: in order to

Что́бы is also how you say what something is *for*. Here there are two patterns, and the choice depends on who does what.

**Same subject in both halves: что́бы + infinitive.**

:::ex
Я пришёл, **что́бы помо́чь**. | I came to help.
Я учу́ ру́сский, **что́бы чита́ть** Че́хова. | I'm learning Russian in order to read Chekhov.
**Что́бы** хорошо́ **говори́ть**, на́до мно́го говори́ть. | To speak well, you have to speak a lot.
:::

**Different subjects: что́бы + subject + past form.**

:::ex
Я говорю́ ме́дленно, **что́бы ты** всё **по́нял**. | I'm speaking slowly so that you understand everything.
Я откры́л окно́, **что́бы бы́ло** не так жа́рко. | I opened the window so it wouldn't be so hot.
Дай мне твой но́мер, **что́бы я мог** тебе́ позвони́ть. | Give me your number so I can call you.
:::

The longer **для того́ что́бы** means exactly the same as что́бы in purpose clauses. It is heavier and more explicit, like "in order to" next to plain "to", and more at home in writing.

:::ex
**Для того́ что́бы** получи́ть ви́зу, ну́жно запо́лнить анке́ту. | In order to get a visa, you need to fill out a form.
:::

After verbs of going and coming, Russian usually skips что́бы and uses a bare infinitive: Я пришёл помо́чь (I've come to help), Она́ пошла́ купи́ть хлеб (She went to buy bread).

### So as not to: что́бы не

The negative purpose is что́бы не. The verb after it is very often perfective, since the point is to prevent one unwanted event.

:::ex
Я записа́л а́дрес, **что́бы не забы́ть**. | I wrote down the address so I wouldn't forget.
Говори́ ти́ше, **что́бы не разбуди́ть** ребёнка. | Keep your voice down so you don't wake the baby.
Я поста́вил буди́льник, **что́бы не проспа́ть**. | I set an alarm so I wouldn't oversleep.
Возьми́ зонт, **что́бы не промо́кнуть**. | Take an umbrella so you don't get soaked.
:::

:::warn Что́бы and что бы
Written as one word, что́бы is the conjunction from this section. Written as two, что бы is just "what" followed by the particle бы: Что бы ты сде́лал? (What would you do?). They sound almost the same, and Russians mix up the spelling often. A quick test: if you can move бы elsewhere in the sentence (Что ты сде́лал бы?), it's two words.
:::

## Putting it together

:::dialogue
А: Что бы ты сде́лал на моём ме́сте? | What would you do in my place?
Б: Я бы поговори́л с нача́льником. | I'd talk to the boss.
А: Я хочу́, что́бы он сам всё по́нял. | I want him to figure it all out himself.
Б: Он не поймёт. Ты бы ему́ написа́ла. | He won't. You should write to him.
А: Е́сли бы э́то бы́ло так про́сто! | If only it were that simple!
:::

:::summary
- **бы + past form** = would / would have. No tense; the verb agrees in gender and number.
- **Е́сли бы** … , … **бы** … : unreal conditions, бы in both halves.
- **Я бы хоте́л**, **ты бы отдохну́л**, **не могли́ бы вы** : softened wishes, advice, requests.
- **Что́бы + subject + past form**: wanting or telling someone else to do something; purpose with a different subject.
- **Что́бы + infinitive**: purpose with the same subject.
- Never a second бы after что́бы, and never a present or future form after it.
:::
