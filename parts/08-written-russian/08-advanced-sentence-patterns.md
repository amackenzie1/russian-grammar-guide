# Advanced sentence patterns

By now you can build any ordinary Russian sentence. What still separates you from an articulate native speaker is a stock of ready-made sentence frames: "the more… the better", "no matter what…", "no sooner had I… than…", "I very nearly…". Each is a small machine made of words you already know (чем, ни, бы, как, так, то), wired together in a way you could not guess from the parts.

None is rare; most turn up in everyday speech as well as in print. Read them through for recognition, then adopt the ones you like.

## Paired frames

### Чем… тем: the more… the more

**Чем** + comparative, **тем** + comparative. The first clause sets the scale, the second moves with it.

:::ex
**Чем** бо́льше, **тем** лу́чше. | The more, the better.
**Чем** ра́ньше вы́йдем, **тем** быстре́е дое́дем. | The earlier we leave, the faster we'll get there.
**Чем** бо́льше я об э́том ду́маю, **тем** ме́ньше понима́ю. | The more I think about it, the less I understand.
**Чем** да́льше в лес, **тем** бо́льше дров. | The deeper into the forest, the more firewood. | proverb: the further you go, the more complicated it gets
:::

A related one: **всё** + comparative means "more and more", a steady drift. Doubling the comparative adds emphasis.

:::ex
Стано́вится **всё холодне́е**. | It's getting colder and colder.
Мы ви́димся **всё ре́же**. | We see each other less and less.
Дела́ иду́т **всё лу́чше и лу́чше**. | Things are going better and better.
:::

### То… то, то ли… то ли, не то… не то

Three look-alikes with different jobs.

**То… то** is alternation: now this, now that.

:::ex
**То** дождь, **то** со́лнце. | Rain one minute, sun the next.
Он **то** смеётся, **то** пла́чет. | He laughs one moment and cries the next.
:::

**То ли… то ли** is ignorance: one of these is true, and I don't know which.

:::ex
**То ли** он забы́л, **то ли** не захоте́л прийти́. | Either he forgot or he didn't feel like coming; I can't tell.
Э́то бы́ло **то ли** в ма́е, **то ли** в ию́не. | It was in May or maybe June.
:::

**Не то… не то** is the same uncertainty with a shrug of vagueness, as if the thing itself were indistinct.

:::ex
На у́лице **не то** дождь, **не то** снег. | Outside it's something between rain and snow.
Она́ **не то** улыба́лась, **не то** собира́лась запла́кать. | She was half smiling, half about to cry.
:::

### Не сто́лько… ско́лько, как… так и

**Не сто́лько** A, **ско́лько** B: "not so much A as B". It shifts the weight from the expected thing to the real one.

:::ex
Я **не сто́лько** уста́л, **ско́лько** проголода́лся. | I'm not so much tired as hungry.
Э́то **не сто́лько** оши́бка, **ско́лько** недоразуме́ние. | It's less a mistake than a misunderstanding.
:::

**Как** A, **так и** B is the formal "both A and B", a staple of journalism.

:::ex
Фильм понра́вился **как** кри́тикам, **так и** зри́телям. | The film pleased both critics and audiences.
Э́то пра́вило де́йствует **как** в Росси́и, **так и** за рубежо́м. | This rule applies both in Russia and abroad.
:::

### Не то что́бы… and ма́ло того́ что…

**Не то что́бы** (or не то что) softens a denial: "it's not that…, exactly". It is usually followed by но or про́сто with the real point.

:::ex
**Не то что́бы** я был про́тив, **но** мне э́то не нра́вится. | It's not that I'm against it, but I don't like it.
Я **не то что́бы** уста́л, **про́сто** не вы́спался. | I'm not tired exactly, I just didn't get enough sleep.
:::

Tacked on after a noun, **не то что** + nominative means "unlike": Ма́ша всегда́ прихо́дит во́время, **не то что ты** ("Masha always comes on time, unlike you").

**Ма́ло того́ что** A, **(так) ещё и** B piles one grievance on another: "as if it weren't enough that…".

:::ex
**Ма́ло того́ что** он опозда́л, **так ещё и** докуме́нты забы́л. | Not only was he late, he forgot the documents on top of that.
:::

## No matter what: concessive ни

Take a question word, add **бы ни**, and put the verb in the past tense form (it is the бы-mood from [Would and so-that](#s-04-07), so it is not really past). The result means "no matter who / what / where / how".

:::ex
**Что бы ни** случи́лось, я с тобо́й. | Whatever happens, I'm with you.
**Где бы** ты **ни** был, звони́. | Wherever you are, call.
**Кто бы ни** звони́л, меня́ нет до́ма. | Whoever calls, I'm not home.
**Ско́лько бы** э́то **ни** сто́ило, я куплю́. | However much it costs, I'll buy it.
**Как бы** я **ни** стара́лся, она́ недово́льна. | However hard I try, she's not satisfied.
:::

Note the placement: бы right after the question word, **ни** right before the verb. The frozen phrase **как бы то [[ns:ни́ было]]** means "be that as it may".

Without бы, the same frame talks about real, usually past or habitual, events. It has a literary or proverbial ring.

:::ex
**Как** я **ни** стара́лся, ничего́ не вы́шло. | Hard as I tried, nothing came of it.
**Что ни** говори́, а он прав. | Say what you like, he's right.
**Куда́ ни** посмо́тришь, везде́ снег. | Wherever you look, there's snow everywhere.
**Ско́лько** его́ **ни** проси́, он не помо́жет. | No matter how much you ask him, he won't help.
:::

:::warn Ни, not не
This is a place where the spelling ни versus не changes the meaning, and Russians themselves stumble over it. With **ни** the clause is concessive: Что он **ни** де́лал, ничего́ не помога́ло ("Whatever he did, nothing helped"). With **не** you get an exclamation that means the opposite of a negative: Чего́ он то́лько **не** де́лал! ("What didn't he do!", that is, he did everything). Likewise Где я то́лько [[ns:не́ был]]! means "Where haven't I been!"
:::

## The instant it happened: timing frames

Russian has several ways to say that one event came hard on the heels of another. All of them end in **как**, which here means "when, and then".

**Не успе́л** + infinitive, **как**…: "hadn't even managed to… when…"

:::ex
**Не успе́л** я войти́, **как** зазвони́л телефо́н. | I had barely walked in when the phone rang.
**Не успе́ли** мы огляну́ться, **как** ле́то ко́нчилось. | Before we knew it, summer was over.
:::

**Сто́ит** (то́лько) + dative + infinitive, **как**…: "all it takes is for… and…". It describes a reliable, often irritating, trigger. Use сто́ило for the past.

:::ex
**Сто́ит** мне сесть за рабо́ту, **как** кто-[[ns:нибудь]] звони́т. | I only have to sit down to work and somebody calls.
**Сто́ило** ему́ уйти́, **как** все заговори́ли. | The moment he left, everyone started talking.
:::

**Едва́**… **как** and **то́лько**… **как**: "scarcely… when". Едва́ is the bookish one.

:::ex
**Едва́** он вошёл, **как** все замолча́ли. | Scarcely had he entered when everyone fell silent.
**То́лько** я засну́л, **как** сосе́ди включи́ли му́зыку. | I had just fallen asleep when the neighbors turned on music.
:::

## Almost, not quite, and about to

### Чуть не: nearly did

**Чуть не** + perfective past says that something very nearly happened and didn't. Despite the не, there is nothing negative about the meaning. **Едва́ не** is its bookish twin.

:::ex
Я **чуть не** упа́л. | I nearly fell.
Мы **чуть не** опозда́ли на самолёт. | We almost missed the plane.
**Чуть не** забы́л: тебе́ звони́ла О́ля. | I almost forgot: Olya called you.
Она́ **едва́ не** запла́кала. | She very nearly burst into tears.
:::

**Чуть ли не** before any word means "practically, all but": Он звони́т **чуть ли не** ка́ждый день ("He calls practically every day").

### Бы́ло: started to, but

An unstressed **бы́ло** after a past-tense verb means the action was begun or intended and then called off. It never changes form, whatever the subject, and a но usually follows.

:::ex
Он **пошёл бы́ло** к две́ри, но переду́мал. | He started toward the door but changed his mind.
Я **хоте́л бы́ло** возрази́ть, но промолча́л. | I was about to object but kept quiet.
Она́ **начала́ бы́ло** чита́ть, но тут пога́с свет. | She had just started reading when the light went out.
:::

This one is mostly literary; in speech people say хоте́л, но…

### Вот-вот, вряд ли

**Вот-вот** + perfective future: it is going to happen any second.

:::ex
По́езд **вот-вот** отпра́вится. | The train is about to leave.
Каза́лось, она́ **вот-вот** запла́чет. | It seemed she would burst into tears any moment.
:::

**Вряд ли** (bookish: **едва́ ли**) means "hardly, it's unlikely that", and can be a whole answer.

:::ex
**Вряд ли** он придёт. | I doubt he'll come.
— Успе́ем? — **Вряд ли.** | "Will we make it?" "Doubt it."
**Едва́ ли** э́то возмо́жно. | That is scarcely possible.
:::

## Так и: three uses of one little pair

**Так и не** + perfective past: the expected thing never happened in the end. There is a note of waiting that came to nothing.

:::ex
Он **так и не** пришёл. | He never did come.
Я **так и не** по́нял, чего́ она́ хоте́ла. | I never did figure out what she wanted.
:::

**Так и** + verb, without не: exactly so, just as stated or expected.

:::ex
Он **так и** сказа́л? | He said exactly that?
Я **так и** знал! | I knew it!
:::

**Так и** + present tense: an urge or a process that can't be held back.

:::ex
**Так и** хо́чется всё бро́сить и уе́хать. | I'm just dying to drop everything and leave.
Слёзы **так и** теку́т. | The tears just keep flowing.
:::

## Wishes, fears and fate

### Infinitive + бы

A bare infinitive with **бы** is a wish sighed aloud, with no "I" and no "want". Negated, it is a hope that something won't happen. With a dative, it becomes gentle advice.

:::ex
**Поспа́ть бы**! | If only I could get some sleep!
Сейча́с **бы** ко́фе. | I could do with a coffee right now. | even the verb can go
**Не опозда́ть бы**. | I hope we're not late.
Тебе́ **бы** отдохну́ть. | You could do with a rest.
:::

**Лишь бы** and **то́лько бы** narrow the wish to one thing that matters: "if only", "as long as". **Вот бы** is a daydream.

:::ex
**Лишь бы** он успе́л. | As long as he makes it.
Ему́ **лишь бы** ничего́ не де́лать. | All he cares about is not doing anything.
**Вот бы** сейча́с на мо́ре! | Wouldn't it be great to be at the seaside right now!
:::

### Как бы не: fear

**Как бы** + **не** + perfective past expresses apprehension: "I hope it doesn't…", "what if…". The не is not a real negation; the speaker is afraid the thing *will* happen. It often follows бою́сь.

:::ex
**Как бы** он **не** опозда́л. | I hope he isn't late. / What if he's late?
Бою́сь, **как бы не** пошёл дождь. | I'm afraid it might rain.
**Как бы** чего́ **не** вы́шло. | Something might go wrong. | the motto of Chekhov's timid "Man in a Case"
:::

### Dative + infinitive: what is bound to be

An infinitive with a dative (or with nothing) and no modal word states what is fated, due or impossible. It sounds terse and final.

:::ex
**Быть** беде́. | There's going to be trouble.
**Не быва́ть** э́тому! | That shall never be!
Нам **не привыка́ть**. | We're used to it. (literally "it's not for us to start getting used to")
Тебе́ **реша́ть**. | It's for you to decide.
Мне за́втра ра́но **встава́ть**. | I have to get up early tomorrow.
:::

### Пусть, пуска́й: granted

You know **пусть** as "let him…" from [the imperative](#s-04-05). It also concedes a point: "all right, suppose so", "even if".

:::ex
**Пусть** так. | So be it. / Granted.
**Пуска́й** говоря́т. | Let them talk.
**Пусть** он не ге́ний, зато́ рабо́тает че́стно. | He may be no genius, but he does honest work.
:::

## Exclaiming and scoffing

**Что за** + nominative asks "what kind of?" or exclaims "what a…!". It is the only place in the language where за is followed by a nominative.

:::ex
**Что** э́то **за** челове́к? | What kind of person is that?
**Что за** шум? | What's that noise?
**Что за** пого́да! | What weather!
:::

**Ну и** + noun is "what a…!" with raised eyebrows; context decides whether it is admiration or dismay.

:::ex
**Ну и** пого́да! | Some weather!
**Ну и** день сего́дня! | What a day!
**Ну и** что? | So what?
:::

**Ещё бы!** is an emphatic "of course!", "I should think so!"

:::ex
— Тебе́ понра́вилось? — **Ещё бы!** | "Did you like it?" "You bet!"
**Ещё бы** он не согласи́лся! | As if he would have said no!
:::

**Куда́** + dative (+ infinitive) dismisses an idea as out of someone's league. It has nothing to do with "where to".

:::ex
**Куда́ мне**! | Me? No chance!
**Куда́ ему́** с тобо́й тяга́ться! | He's no match for you!
**Куда́ тебе́** сто́лько де́нег? | What do you need that much money for?
:::

A **genitive with nothing else**, said with feeling, exclaims at a quantity. With mass nouns it is often the second genitive in -у from [Irregular nouns and loose ends](#s-08-06).

:::ex
**Наро́ду**-то! | So many people!
**Сне́гу** намело́! | Look how much snow has piled up!
**Де́нег** у него́! | He's rolling in money!
:::

**Rhetorical questions** with **не… ли** and **же** assert the opposite of what they ask. Не… ли expects "yes"; a question word plus же expects "obviously nobody / nothing".

:::ex
**Не** ты **ли** э́то говори́л? | Wasn't it you who said that?
**Не** пора́ **ли** нам домо́й? | Isn't it time we went home?
**Не** лу́чше **ли** подожда́ть? | Wouldn't it be better to wait?
Кто **же** э́того не зна́ет? | Who doesn't know that? (Everyone does.)
:::

## Connecting thoughts

**Так что**: "so" as a consequence, lighter than поэ́тому and often used to wrap up.

:::ex
У меня́ мно́го рабо́ты, **так что** я не приду́. | I have a lot of work, so I'm not coming.
:::

**Тем бо́лее** (что): "all the more (since)", "especially as".

:::ex
Я никуда́ не пойду́, **тем бо́лее что** идёт дождь. | I'm not going anywhere, especially since it's raining.
— Он же ничего́ не зна́ет. — **Тем бо́лее!** | "But he doesn't know anything." "All the more reason!"
:::

**Не говоря́ уже́ о** + prepositional: "not to mention", "let alone".

:::ex
У него́ нет вре́мени да́же на сон, **не говоря́ уже́ о** спо́рте. | He doesn't even have time to sleep, let alone exercise.
:::

**Ра́зве что**: "except perhaps", a grudging exception.

:::ex
Ничего́ не измени́лось, **ра́зве что** це́ны вы́росли. | Nothing has changed, except perhaps that prices have gone up.
:::

**Что́бы не сказа́ть**: "not to say", for putting a stronger word on the table while pretending not to.

:::ex
Иде́я сме́лая, **что́бы не сказа́ть** безу́мная. | The idea is bold, not to say insane.
:::

## Storytelling moves

Two last devices belong to narration, from fairy tales to jokes to telling a friend about your day.

**Verb first.** Neutral order puts the subject before the verb. Putting the verb first signals "here begins a story" or "and next…". It is the opening of every folk tale and every joke.

:::ex
**Жил-был** стари́к. | Once upon a time there lived an old man.
**Жи́ли-бы́ли** дед да ба́ба. | Once upon a time there lived an old man and an old woman.
**Захо́дит** мужи́к в бар… | A guy walks into a bar…
**Пришёл** он домо́й и ви́дит: дверь откры́та. | So he came home, and what does he see: the door is open.
:::

**The present for the past.** As that last example shows, Russian storytellers slip into the present tense to make past events vivid, far more freely than English does. It is normal in conversation and in written history alike.

:::ex
**Сижу́** я вчера́ до́ма, никого́ не тро́гаю, и вдруг **звони́т** Ди́ма. | So I'm sitting at home yesterday, minding my own business, and suddenly Dima calls.
В 1812 году́ Наполео́н **вхо́дит** в Москву́. | In 1812 Napoleon enters Moscow.
:::

Because a present-tense verb has no aspect choice to make (it is always imperfective), this trick also gives the narrator a rest from deciding between process and result. The listener supplies the sequence.

With these frames and the written forms of this part, there is very little in a Russian newspaper or novel whose grammar you have not met. What remains is vocabulary, and the [Reference](#s-09-01) part for looking things up.
