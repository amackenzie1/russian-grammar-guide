# Plurals

English makes plurals by adding "-s". Russian swaps the ending of the noun for a plural one, chosen by the noun's gender and by whether its last consonant is hard or soft. The core fits in one small table. Then come very common nouns that go their own way, as "children", "men" and "feet" do in English; those you learn one by one.

In the plural, gender stops mattering to the surrounding words. The three words for "it" collapse into a single "they", **они́**:

:::ex
Где ключи́? — Вот они́. | Where are the keys? — Here they are.
:::

:::vocab
журна́л | magazine | m.
ко́мната | room | f.
уро́к | lesson | m.
де́вушка | young woman, girl | f.
ключ | key | m.
го́род | city | m.
глаз | eye | m.
по́езд | train | m.
оте́ц | father | m.
ребёнок | child | m.; pl. де́ти
челове́к | person | m.; pl. лю́ди
стул | chair | m.
я́блоко | apple | n.
де́ньги | money | pl. only
часы́ | clock, watch | pl. only
:::

## The regular pattern

| | Singular ends in | Plural | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine | hard consonant | add **-ы** | стол → столы́, журна́л → журна́лы |
| masculine | **-й** or **-ь** | replace with **-и** | музе́й → музе́и, слова́рь → словари́ |
| feminine | **-а** | **-ы** | ко́мната → ко́мнаты, маши́на → маши́ны |
| feminine | **-я** or **-ь** | **-и** | неде́ля → неде́ли, дверь → две́ри |
| neuter | **-о** | **-а** | сло́во → слова́, письмо́ → пи́сьма |
| neuter | **-е** | **-я** | мо́ре → моря́, зда́ние → зда́ния |

Masculine and feminine nouns share one ending in two spellings: **-ы** after a hard consonant, **-и** after a soft one (the pairing from [Hard and soft consonants](#s-01-02)). Neuter nouns have their own ending, **-а**, with **-я** as its soft twin.

:::ex
студе́нт → студе́нты | student → students
у́лица → у́лицы | street → streets
трамва́й → трамва́и | tram → trams
гость → го́сти | guest → guests
пе́сня → пе́сни | song → songs
фотогра́фия → фотогра́фии | photo → photos
ночь → но́чи | night → nights
ме́сто → места́ | place → places
пла́тье → пла́тья | dress → dresses
:::

### The spelling rule steps in

By the [spelling rules](#s-01-05), Russian never writes **ы** after **г, к, х, ж, ч, ш, щ**, only **и**. A huge number of nouns end in these consonants, especially **к**, and for all of them the plural is written **-и**, even where the consonant is hard:

:::ex
кни́га → кни́ги | book → books
уро́к → уро́ки | lesson → lessons
де́вушка → де́вушки | young woman → young women
подру́га → подру́ги | (female) friend → friends
язы́к → языки́ | language → languages
нож → ножи́ | knife → knives
ключ → ключи́ | key → keys
каранда́ш → карандаши́ | pencil → pencils
:::

Every noun ending in **-ка** works this way, and there are thousands: **студе́нтка → студе́нтки**, **ча́шка → ча́шки** (cups), **су́мка → су́мки** (bags), **ба́бушка → ба́бушки**.

## Stress likes to move

The stress does not always stay put: **стол → столы́**, **сло́во → слова́**, **письмо́ → пи́сьма**. Stress shift between singular and plural is common in short, everyday nouns, and it is the hardest part of the plural. No rule predicts it from the spelling, but there are patterns.

**Two-syllable neuter nouns usually flip.** If the stress is on the ending in the singular, it moves to the stem in the plural, and the other way around:

:::ex
окно́ → о́кна | window → windows
письмо́ → пи́сьма | letter → letters
лицо́ → ли́ца | face → faces
вино́ → ви́на | wine → wines
яйцо́ → я́йца | egg → eggs
сло́во → слова́ | word → words
де́ло → дела́ | matter, business → matters
ме́сто → места́ | place → places
мо́ре → моря́ | sea → seas
по́ле → поля́ | field → fields
:::

Unstressed final **-о** and **-а** sound the same, so stress is often the only thing that tells your ear whether it is hearing one **сло́во** or several **слова́**. Longer neuter nouns generally keep their stress where it was: **зда́ние → зда́ния**, **упражне́ние → упражне́ния** (exercises). So do some two-syllable ones, such as **кре́сло → кре́сла** (armchairs).

**Many feminine nouns stressed on the ending pull the stress back in the plural:**

:::ex
сестра́ → сёстры | sister → sisters
жена́ → жёны | wife → wives
страна́ → стра́ны | country → countries
рука́ → ру́ки | hand, arm → hands
нога́ → но́ги | foot, leg → feet
голова́ → го́ловы | head → heads
река́ → ре́ки | river → rivers
гора́ → го́ры | mountain → mountains
игра́ → и́гры | game → games
семья́ → се́мьи | family → families
:::

The letter **ё** only ever appears under stress, so when the stress lands on an **е** that was "really" **ё** all along, the dots appear: **сестра́ → сёстры**, **жена́ → жёны**.

**Many short masculine nouns push the stress onto the ending:** **стол → столы́**, **врач → врачи́**, **сад → сады́** (gardens), **мост → мосты́** (bridges), **слова́рь → словари́**, **рубль → рубли́**.

For any other noun, assume the stress stays where it is. Native speakers argue about some of these themselves.

## Masculine plurals in stressed -а́

A group of masculine nouns, many of them extremely common, take a plural in **-а́** (or **-я́** after a soft consonant) and not **-ы**. The ending is always stressed, which makes these easy to recognize by ear.

:::ex
дом → дома́ | house → houses
го́род → города́ | city → cities
глаз → глаза́ | eye → eyes
по́езд → поезда́ | train → trains
па́спорт → паспорта́ | passport → passports
но́мер → номера́ | number, hotel room → numbers
а́дрес → адреса́ | address → addresses
ве́чер → вечера́ | evening → evenings
лес → леса́ | forest → forests
бе́рег → берега́ | shore, bank → shores
о́стров → острова́ | island → islands
цвет → цвета́ | color → colors
го́лос → голоса́ | voice → voices
до́ктор → доктора́ | doctor → doctors
профе́ссор → профессора́ | professor → professors
учи́тель → учителя́ | teacher → teachers
:::

You cannot predict which nouns belong to this group, although words for paired things (**глаза́**, **берега́**, **рукава́** "sleeves") and for professions are well represented. The group has been growing for centuries, and for some words both plurals are in use.

:::warn до́ма and дома́
**До́ма**, stressed on the first syllable, is "at home". **Дома́**, stressed on the end, is "houses". Without stress marks the two look identical, and context tells them apart: **Я до́ма** (I'm home), **Э́то дома́** (These are houses).
:::

## Disappearing vowels

In some nouns the last vowel of the stem, an **о** or **е**, drops out as soon as an ending is added. Grammars call these fleeting vowels: the vowel makes the bare form pronounceable and is not needed once an ending follows.

:::ex
оте́ц → отцы́ | father → fathers
день → дни | day → days
пода́рок → пода́рки | present → presents
звоно́к → звонки́ | phone call, bell → calls
кусо́к → куски́ | piece → pieces
америка́нец → америка́нцы | American → Americans
па́лец → па́льцы | finger → fingers
у́гол → углы́ | corner → corners
ого́нь → огни́ | fire, light → lights
сон → сны | dream → dreams
:::

This happens almost exclusively in masculine nouns ending in **-ок**, **-ец** and **-ень**, plus a few short words. It is not automatic: **уро́к → уро́ки** and **игро́к → игроки́** (players) keep their vowel. Fleeting vowels return with the cases, because they vanish before *any* ending, not just the plural one.

## The irregular ones

As in English, the oldest and most basic words misbehave. Learn these now; they are in constant use.

**Plural in -ья.** A handful of masculine nouns (and a few neuter ones) take **-ья**, sometimes with a change in the stem:

:::ex
друг → друзья́ | friend → friends
брат → бра́тья | brother → brothers
сын → сыновья́ | son → sons
муж → мужья́ | husband → husbands
стул → сту́лья | chair → chairs
лист → ли́стья | leaf → leaves
де́рево → дере́вья | tree → trees
:::

**A different word altogether:**

:::ex
челове́к → лю́ди | person → people
ребёнок → де́ти | child → children
:::

**Mother and daughter** grow an extra syllable, **-ер-**, which is the same one hiding in English "mother" and "daughter":

:::ex
мать → ма́тери | mother → mothers
дочь → до́чери | daughter → daughters
:::

**-и where you would expect something else:**

:::ex
я́блоко → я́блоки | apple → apples | neuter, but -и
у́хо → у́ши | ear → ears
плечо́ → пле́чи | shoulder → shoulders
коле́но → коле́ни | knee → knees
сосе́д → сосе́ди | neighbor → neighbors | soft -и after a hard д
:::

**Nouns in -ин lose it.** Words for people ending in **-анин / -янин** drop the **-ин** and end in **-е**: **англича́нин → англича́не** (Englishman → the English), **россия́нин → россия́не** (citizens of Russia), **граждани́н → гра́ждане** (citizens).

**The -мя nouns** lengthen: **и́мя → имена́** (names), **вре́мя → времена́** (times).

**One more:** **цвето́к → цветы́** (flower → flowers). Compare **цвет → цвета́** (color → colors) above.

Borrowed nouns that never change, like **такси́**, **кафе́**, **пальто́** and **метро́**, do not change in the plural either; the words around them show the number.

## Nouns that are only plural, or only singular

Some nouns exist only in the plural, as "scissors" and "jeans" do in English. Many are things made of two parts.

:::ex
де́ньги | money
часы́ | clock, watch
очки́ | glasses
брю́ки | trousers
джи́нсы | jeans
но́жницы | scissors
воро́та | gate
кани́кулы | school vacation
:::

These take **они́**, even when there is one object: **Где очки́? — Вот они́.** Russian money "are", not "is": **Где де́ньги? — Они́ здесь.**

**Часы́** is simply the plural of **час** (hour), and it serves for any timepiece, from a wristwatch to a tower clock.

Substances and abstractions normally have no plural: **молоко́** (milk), **вода́** (water), **са́хар** (sugar), **рис** (rice), **мя́со** (meat), **любо́вь** (love), **пого́да** (weather).

Russian goes further than English here. Some things that English counts are an uncountable mass with a singular name, particularly vegetables, berries and categories of things:

:::ex
карто́шка | potatoes
морко́вь | carrots | f.
лук | onions
виногра́д | grapes
клубни́ка | strawberries
оде́жда | clothes
о́бувь | shoes, footwear | f.
посу́да | dishes
ме́бель | furniture | f.
:::

A kilo of "potatoes" is a kilo of singular **карто́шка**.

## Seeing it in sentences

Plural nouns fit the sentences of the first section unchanged. **Э́то** stays **э́то**, and there is still no word for "are":

:::ex
Э́то студе́нты. | These are students.
Э́то не я́блоки, а помидо́ры. | Those aren't apples, they're tomatoes.
Де́ти до́ма? | Are the kids at home?
Вот ключи́ и докуме́нты. | Here are the keys and the documents.
А́ня и Ма́ша — сёстры. | Anya and Masha are sisters.
Кто они́? — Они́ врачи́. | Who are they? — They're doctors.
Лю́ди здесь, а маши́ны там. | The people are here and the cars are over there.
:::

These are the dictionary-form plurals, used for the subject of a sentence and after **э́то**. Plural nouns have other forms for other jobs in the sentence; see [Cases in the plural](#s-03-07). A fuller list of irregular plurals is in [Irregular nouns and loose ends](#s-08-06).
