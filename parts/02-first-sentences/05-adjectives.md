# Adjectives

A Russian adjective is one more word that has to match its noun. You have seen the idea twice already, with **мой / моё / моя́ / мои́** and **э́тот / э́то / э́та / э́ти**. Adjectives do the same thing with their own, slightly longer endings: one for masculine, one for neuter, one for feminine, one for plural.

The endings look like a lot at first, because textbooks list three or four "types" of adjective. There is really one set of endings. The variety comes from two things you already know from Part 1: every ending has a hard and a soft spelling, and the [spelling rules](#s-01-05) forbid certain letter combinations. Keep that in mind and the tables below become one table seen from different angles.

:::vocab
но́вый | new
ста́рый | old
краси́вый | beautiful
интере́сный | interesting
вку́сный | tasty
молодо́й | young
большо́й | big
ма́ленький | small
хоро́ший | good
плохо́й | bad
ру́сский | Russian
дорого́й | expensive; dear
горя́чий | hot | to the touch: food, water
си́ний | (dark) blue
после́дний | last
о́чень | very
:::

## The standard endings

The dictionary form of an adjective is the masculine singular. Take **но́вый** (new): the stem is **нов-** and the ending is **-ый**. Swap the ending to match the noun:

| | Ending | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine | **-ый** | но́в**ый** дом | a new house |
| neuter | **-ое** | но́в**ое** окно́ | a new window |
| feminine | **-ая** | но́в**ая** маши́на | a new car |
| plural | **-ые** | но́в**ые** дома́ | new houses |

Each ending is two letters, and the first of them should look familiar. Feminine **-ая** starts with the **а** of feminine nouns; neuter **-ое** starts with the **о** of neuter nouns; plural **-ые** starts with the **ы** of the plural. Adjectives rhyme with their nouns, more or less: **но́вая маши́на**, **но́вое окно́**, **но́вые журна́лы**.

As with possessives, the plural has one form for all genders.

:::ex
ста́рый друг | an old friend
краси́вая у́лица | a beautiful street
интере́сное ме́сто | an interesting place
вку́сный суп | tasty soup
но́вые друзья́ | new friends
ста́рые фотогра́фии | old photos
:::

The adjective follows the gender of the noun, not its last letter. So it is **ста́рый де́душка** (old grandpa) and **мой но́вый колле́га** for a male colleague; **ста́рая дверь** but **ста́рый слова́рь**; **но́вое и́мя**; **вку́сный ко́фе**.

### When the ending is stressed: -о́й

Some adjectives are stressed on the ending. For them, the masculine form is **-о́й**, not **-ый**. Nothing else changes:

| masculine | neuter | feminine | plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| молод**о́й** | молод**о́е** | молод**а́я** | молод**ы́е** |

**Молодо́й** (young) is the model; **плохо́й** (bad), **дорого́й** (expensive), **большо́й** (big), **друго́й** (other, different), **просто́й** (simple) and **родно́й** (native, one's own) are stressed the same way. If the dictionary form ends in **-ой**, the stress is on the ending in every form.

:::ex
молодо́й челове́к | a young man
молода́я семья́ | a young family
просто́е сло́во | a simple word
молоды́е лю́ди | young people
:::

## Soft adjectives

A smaller group of adjectives has a stem ending in a soft consonant, nearly always a soft **н**. Their endings are the same four, written with the soft-series vowel letters: **и** for **ы**, **е** for **о**, **я** for **а**.

| | hard | soft |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | но́в**ый** | си́н**ий** |
| neuter | но́в**ое** | си́н**ее** |
| feminine | но́в**ая** | си́н**яя** |
| plural | но́в**ые** | си́н**ие** |

:::ex
си́ний костю́м | a blue suit
си́нее мо́ре | the blue sea
си́няя маши́на | a blue car
си́ние глаза́ | blue eyes
после́дний день | the last day
после́дняя неде́ля | the last week
дома́шнее зада́ние | homework | literally "home task"
:::

Soft adjectives are few, and most of them have to do with time and place: **после́дний** (last), **дома́шний** (home, domestic), **сосе́дний** (neighboring, next-door), **у́тренний** (morning), **вече́рний** (evening), **сего́дняшний** (today's), **ле́тний** (summer), **зи́мний** (winter), **ве́рхний** (upper), **ни́жний** (lower), **ли́шний** (spare, unnecessary). If an adjective ends in **-ний**, it is soft. **Си́ний** is the only everyday color in the group.

## The spelling rules make "mixed" adjectives

Now the part that makes the system look more complicated than it is. Two spelling rules interfere with the standard endings:

1. After **г, к, х, ж, ч, ш, щ**, write **и**, never **ы**.
2. After **ж, ч, ш, щ, ц**, an unstressed **о** in an ending is written **е**.

Adjectives whose stem ends in one of these consonants take the ordinary hard endings, adjusted wherever a rule says so. That gives three very common patterns.

**Stem in г, к, х** (like **ру́сский**): rule 1 changes **-ый** to **-ий** and **-ые** to **-ие**. The other two endings are untouched.

**Stem in ж, ч, ш, щ, stress on the stem** (like **хоро́ший**): rule 1 does the same, and rule 2 turns **-ое** into **-ее**. The feminine is still the hard **-ая**.

**Stem in any of these, stress on the ending** (like **большо́й**): masculine is **-о́й**, as for any end-stressed adjective. The **о** is stressed, so rule 2 does not apply, and only the plural is affected.

| | ру́сский | хоро́ший | большо́й |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine | ру́сск**ий** | хоро́ш**ий** | больш**о́й** |
| neuter | ру́сск**ое** | хоро́ш**ее** | больш**о́е** |
| feminine | ру́сск**ая** | хоро́ш**ая** | больш**а́я** |
| plural | ру́сск**ие** | хоро́ш**ие** | больш**и́е** |

None of this needs to be memorized as a separate type. If you know the standard endings and the two spelling rules, these forms write themselves. The thing to watch is the feminine: **хоро́шая** and **ру́сская** with **-ая**, but **си́няя** with **-яя**. **Хоро́ший** looks soft in the dictionary; it isn't.

Like **ру́сский**: **ма́ленький** (small), **англи́йский** (English), **лёгкий** (light, easy), **ти́хий** (quiet), **высо́кий** (tall, high), **стро́гий** (strict), and every adjective in **-ский**. Like **хоро́ший**: **горя́чий** (hot), **све́жий** (fresh), **о́бщий** (shared, general), **ста́рший** (elder), **мла́дший** (younger), **сле́дующий** (next). Like **большо́й**: **плохо́й**, **дорого́й**, **друго́й**, **чужо́й** (someone else's), **городско́й** (city, urban).

:::ex
ру́сский язы́к | the Russian language
ру́сская ку́хня | Russian cuisine
ма́ленькие де́ти | small children
хоро́ший вопро́с | a good question
хоро́шее ме́сто | a good spot
хоро́шая иде́я | a good idea
горя́чий чай | hot tea
све́жее молоко́ | fresh milk
большо́й го́род | a big city
больша́я семья́ | a big family
больши́е де́ньги | big money
ста́ршая сестра́ и мла́дший брат | an older sister and a younger brother
:::

:::note No capital letters
Adjectives of nationality, and the names of languages, are written with a small letter: **ру́сский**, **англи́йский**, **америка́нский**. So are the days of the week and the months.
:::

## Before the noun or after it

Where you put the adjective changes what the sentence says.

**Before the noun**, the adjective is part of the description, and you have a phrase, not yet a sentence: **но́вый дом**, "a new house". To make a sentence you need something else, such as **э́то**:

:::ex
Э́то но́вый дом. | This is a new house.
Э́то хоро́шая иде́я. | That's a good idea.
Ма́ша — моя́ ста́рая подру́га. | Masha is an old friend of mine.
:::

**After the noun**, the adjective is what you are saying about it. Since there is no "is", noun-then-adjective is a complete sentence:

:::ex
Дом но́вый. | The house is new.
Иде́я хоро́шая. | The idea is good.
Чай горя́чий. | The tea is hot.
Де́ти ещё ма́ленькие. | The children are still small.
:::

In speech there is a tiny pause after the noun, and the voice comes down on the adjective. The agreement is exactly the same in both positions.

Combine this with the difference between **э́то** and **э́тот** from the last section and you get two ways of saying nearly the same thing:

:::ex
Э́то интере́сная кни́га. | This is an interesting book.
Э́та кни́га интере́сная. | This book is interesting.
Э́то о́чень дорого́й рестора́н. | This is a very expensive restaurant.
Э́тот рестора́н о́чень дорого́й. | This restaurant is very expensive.
Мой брат ещё молодо́й. | My brother is still young.
На́ша кварти́ра ма́ленькая, но хоро́шая. | Our apartment is small but nice.
:::

**О́чень** (very) goes in front of the adjective and never changes.

:::note Short forms exist
Many adjectives also have a short form used only after the noun (**дом нов**, **она́ краси́ва**). In everyday speech the long forms you are learning here are the normal choice, except for a small set of words like **рад** (glad) and **гото́в** (ready). They are covered in [Short adjectives and predicate words](#s-06-08).
:::

## Како́й: what kind of, which

The question word that asks for an adjective is **како́й**. It is itself an adjective of the **большо́й** type: **како́й, како́е, кака́я, каки́е**.

:::dialogue
А: Како́й э́то го́род? | What city is this?
Б: Э́то Каза́нь. | It's Kazan.
А: Кака́я она́? | What is it like?
Б: Больша́я и о́чень краси́вая. | Big and very beautiful.
А: Како́е твоё люби́мое ме́сто? | What's your favorite spot?
Б: Ста́рый центр. | The old center.
:::

**Како́й** covers both "what kind of?" and "which?". It agrees with the noun it asks about, which is why the second question is **кака́я**: **Каза́нь** is one of those feminine nouns in **-ь**.

It is also how Russian exclaims. English says "What a…!"; Russian uses **како́й** with a falling, emphatic tone:

:::ex
Како́й краси́вый го́род! | What a beautiful city!
Кака́я хоро́шая пого́да! | What nice weather!
Како́е вку́сное моро́женое! | What delicious ice cream!
Каки́е лю́ди! | Look who's here! | literally "what people!", said on running into friends
:::

## Тако́й: such, so

**Тако́й** is the answer-shaped partner of **како́й**: "like this", "such". It declines the same way: **тако́й, тако́е, така́я, таки́е**. Its most common use is to intensify an adjective, where English says "so" or "such a":

:::ex
Он тако́й у́мный! | He's so smart!
Она́ така́я молода́я. | She's so young.
Э́то тако́й хоро́ший фильм! | It's such a good film!
Таки́е краси́вые цветы́! | Such beautiful flowers!
Како́й вопро́с, тако́й и отве́т. | Ask a silly question, get a silly answer. | literally "what kind of question, such the answer"
:::

English "so" does not change, but **тако́й** must agree. The difference from **о́чень** is the same as between "so" and "very": **о́чень** states a degree, **тако́й** is more emotional.

## Adjectives that became nouns

Some words have adjective endings but are used as nouns. Usually a noun has dropped away and the adjective is carrying on alone. They keep agreeing as adjectives, with the gender of the noun that disappeared (or of the person meant).

:::ex
ру́сский, ру́сская, ру́сские | a Russian (man), a Russian (woman), Russians
знако́мый, знако́мая | an acquaintance (male, female)
взро́слый, взро́слые | an adult, adults
столо́вая | dining room; canteen | f., from "ко́мната"
ва́нная | bathroom | f.
моро́женое | ice cream | n., literally "frozen"
бу́дущее | the future | n.
выходно́й | day off | m., from "день"
:::

:::ex
Он ру́сский, а она́ америка́нка. | He's Russian and she's American.
Э́то мой ста́рый знако́мый. | This is an old acquaintance of mine.
Где здесь столо́вая? | Where's the canteen around here?
Э́то вку́сное моро́женое. | This is good ice cream.
:::

**Ру́сский** is the only nationality that works this way. All the others are ordinary nouns, usually in a masculine and feminine pair: **америка́нец / америка́нка**, **англича́нин / англича́нка**, **не́мец / не́мка** (German). Their matching adjectives (**америка́нский**, **англи́йский**, **неме́цкий**) describe things, not people: **америка́нский фильм**, but **он америка́нец**.

Russian surnames in **-ский** and **-о́й** are adjectives too, which is why they have feminine forms: **Достое́вский** and his wife **Достое́вская**, **Толсто́й** and **Толста́я**.

## One sentence, everything agreeing

The grammar of this part so far comes together in sentences like these. Every word that can agree with the noun does:

:::ex
Э́то мой но́вый телефо́н. | This is my new phone.
Э́то моя́ но́вая рабо́та. | This is my new job.
Э́то моё но́вое пальто́. | This is my new coat.
Э́то мои́ но́вые друзья́. | These are my new friends.
Э́тот ста́рый дом — наш. | This old house is ours.
Э́та ма́ленькая соба́ка — ва́ша? | Is this little dog yours?
Каки́е э́то я́блоки? — Не зна́ю, но о́чень вку́сные. | What kind of apples are these? — I don't know, but they're very tasty.
:::

(**Не зна́ю**, "I don't know", is a verb form, borrowed from the next section because you will need it daily.)

The forms here are again only the dictionary-form set. Adjectives change for case along with their nouns; the full tables are in [Adjective, pronoun and determiner tables](#s-09-02).
