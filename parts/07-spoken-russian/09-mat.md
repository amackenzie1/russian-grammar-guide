# Мат: Russian obscenity

:::warn Content note
This section prints Russian obscenities in full and translates them with English ones. It is here because you cannot understand real spoken Russian, or a great deal of its film, music and internet, without recognizing these words. Read it for understanding first; whether and when to use any of it is covered at the end.
:::

Most languages swear with a loose assortment of rude words. Russian has something more organized: a small, closed set of roots, called **мат**, with the entire machinery of Russian word formation bolted on. From four roots the language builds hundreds of nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs, enough to say nearly anything.

Its social position is unlike that of English swearing. In public it is much more strictly off limits: bleeped, kept out of print, restricted by law. In private it is often every bit as casual as "fuck" is among English-speaking friends. Both halves of that are true at once, and the last part of this section is about living with the gap.

Мат is a grammar topic and not only a vocabulary list. If you have worked through [verbal prefixes](#s-05-06) and [word formation](#s-08-07), you already own every tool it uses. This section shows how the system is put together, so that what you hear stops being noise.

:::vocab
мат | the obscene vocabulary itself | m., no plural
матери́ться | to swear using мат | impf.
руга́ться ма́том | to swear using мат | literally "to curse with мат"
ма́терный | obscene (of a word, a joke) | ма́терное сло́во
нецензу́рная брань | "uncensorable abuse" | the legal and official term
выража́ться | to use bad language | literally "to express oneself"; Не выража́йтесь!
:::

The name comes from мать (mother): the oldest and most notorious curse in the set is an insult aimed at the listener's mother.

## The four roots

By the usual count, and by the one Russia's media regulator uses, мат is four roots and everything derived from them.

| Root word | Literal meaning | English counterpart |
|---|---|---|
| **хуй** | penis | dick, cock |
| **пизда́** | vulva | cunt |
| **еба́ть** | to fuck | fuck |
| **блядь** | whore | as an insult, "whore"; as an exclamation, "fuck!" |

A few more rude words hover at the edge: **су́ка** (bitch), **муда́к** (a contemptible man, something like "asshole"), **говно́** (shit), **жо́па** (ass), **срать** (to shit). They are vulgar, but most Russians don't feel them as мат proper, and they are bleeped less consistently. Су́ка deserves a special mention because it is everywhere, usually not as an insult to anyone but as a free-floating exclamation, alone or welded to блядь:

:::ex
Су́ка, опя́ть дождь. | Fuck, it's raining again.
Су́ка, блядь, где ключи́? | For fuck's sake, where are the keys?
:::

The four words decline and conjugate like any others. Хуй is an ordinary masculine noun (ху́я, ху́ю, ху́ем; several fixed phrases stress the ending instead, as in ни хуя́ and на хуя́), пизда́ an ordinary feminine one (пизды́, пизде́, пизду́), and еба́ть conjugates ебу́, ебёшь, ебу́т, past еба́л. The grammar is regular; only the vocabulary is unprintable.

## What it is actually used for

Here is the first surprise. In everyday use these words rarely refer to sex or anatomy, and most of the time they are not even insults. Their job is expressive: they mark that the speaker is angry, delighted, amazed, exhausted or simply relaxed enough not to bother being polite. A sentence in мат usually has a perfectly neutral twin that says the same thing without the charge.

:::ex
Ни хуя́ не понима́ю. | I don't understand a fucking thing. | neutral: Ничего́ не понима́ю.
Мне по́хуй. | I don't give a fuck. | neutral: Мне всё равно́.
Э́то пизде́ц. | This is a fucking disaster. | neutral: Э́то кошма́р.
Я заеба́лся. | I'm fucking exhausted. | neutral: Я о́чень уста́л.
Фильм охуе́нный. | The movie is fucking amazing. | neutral: Фильм отли́чный.
:::

Notice the last one. Мат expresses delight as easily as fury, sometimes from the same root: **хуёвый** is "lousy", while **охуе́нный** is "amazing". **Пизде́ц** is a catastrophe; **пизда́тый** is "excellent"; **заеби́сь** is "fucking great". What the root contributes is intensity. The direction comes from the suffix and the situation.

## The ladder of rudeness

For nearly every мат expression Russian keeps a set of weaker versions, made by swapping the root for a harmless word that starts with the same sound. Хуй gives way to **хрен** (horseradish), **хер** (an old name of the letter х) or **фиг** (fig); блядь to **блин** (pancake); пизде́ц to **пипе́ц**, **капе́ц** or **песе́ц** (arctic fox). The frame around the root stays exactly the same, so if you know one rung you can read all of them.

| Meaning | Neutral | Crude but common | Rude | Мат |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nothing | ничего́ | ни фига́ | ни хрена́ | ни хуя́ |
| I don't care | мне всё равно́ | мне по́фиг | мне по́хрен | мне по́хуй |
| a lot | мно́го | дофига́ | до хрена́ | дохуя́ |
| nonsense, stuff | ерунда́ | фигня́ | хрень | хуйня́ |
| who knows | кто зна́ет | фиг зна́ет | хрен зна́ет | хуй зна́ет |
| what for? | заче́м? | на фига́? | на хрена́? | на хуя́? |
| badly | пло́хо | фиго́во | хрено́во | хуёво |
| great | отли́чно | офиге́нно | охрене́нно | охуе́нно |
| excellent! | отли́чно! | зашиби́сь! | — | заеби́сь! |
| to be stunned | удиви́ться | офиге́ть | охрене́ть | охуе́ть |
| disaster | кошма́р | капе́ц | пипе́ц, песе́ц | пизде́ц |
| I'm sick of it | мне надое́ло | меня́ доста́ло | меня́ задолба́ло | меня́ заеба́ло |
| leave me alone | отста́нь | отвяжи́сь | отвали́ | отъеби́сь |
| damn! | ой! | блин!, чёрт! | — | блядь! |

The middle columns are the ones you will hear in offices, on buses and from perfectly respectable people. A Russian who says «ни фига́ себе́!» or «по́лный песе́ц» knows exactly what the stand-in is standing in for, and so does everyone listening. That is the point: the feeling is delivered and the taboo is technically kept.

The supply of stand-ins is large and inventive. Besides the ones in the table you will meet **трынде́ц** and **звезде́ц** for пизде́ц, **бля́ха-му́ха** for блядь, and a whole family of exclamations that begin with the first sound of the "mother" curse and then swerve somewhere innocent: **ёлки-па́лки**, **ё-моё**, **ёшкин кот**, **ёкарный баба́й**, **япо́на мать**.

## The grammar: one root, every prefix

The verb еба́ть is where the system shows off. Nobody uses the bare verb much. What is used constantly is the root with a prefix, and each prefix contributes the same meaning it has in ordinary verbs. If you know the neutral verb in the middle column, you can decode the obscene one.

| Мат verb | Built like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| заеба́ть | заму́чить, замота́ть | to wear out, to drive up the wall |
| заеба́ться | заму́читься | to be worn out |
| наеба́ть | наду́ть, обману́ть | to cheat, to rip off |
| проеба́ть | пропусти́ть, потеря́ть | to lose, to waste, to blow (a chance) |
| отъеба́ться | отста́ть, отвяза́ться | to leave someone alone |
| доеба́ться | докопа́ться, приста́ть | to pester, to pick on |
| выёбываться | выпе́ндриваться | to show off |
| съеба́ться | смы́ться | to clear out, to run off |
| разъеба́ть | разби́ть | to smash to pieces |
| въеба́ть, уеба́ть | вре́зать, уда́рить | to hit hard |

:::ex
Ты заеба́л! | I've fucking had it with you!
Нас наеба́ли. | We got screwed over. | cheated
Я проеба́л все де́ньги. | I blew all the money.
Отъеби́сь от меня́! | Get the fuck away from me!
Не выёбывайся. | Stop showing off.
:::

The prefixes за- (to exhaustion), на- (onto someone, to their cost), про- (past, missed), от- (away from) and вы- (out, on display) are doing just what they did in the [prefix section](#s-05-06). Even the spelling behaves: prefixes ending in a consonant take a hard sign before the е (отъ-, съ-, разъ-, въ-), as they do in отъе́хать and съесть.

Two more verbs from the same root are worth knowing. The reflexive **еба́ться с** + instrumental means to struggle with something fiddly and tedious. The one-off verb **ёбнуться**, built with the "single action" suffix -ну-, means to lose your mind, or to fall or hit yourself hard.

:::ex
Я весь день еба́лся с э́тим отчётом. | I spent the whole day fucking around with this report.
Ты ёбнулся? | Have you lost your fucking mind?
:::

The root also makes adjectives. **Ёбаный** and **ебу́чий** are the all-purpose "fucking" before a noun (ёбаный дождь); **ебану́тый** means crazy; and **охуе́вший**, from the хуй root, describes someone who has become outrageously insolent.

The other roots are productive too, in a smaller way:

- From **пизда́**: **пизде́ц** (disaster; also "extremely", as in пизде́ц как хо́лодно, "fucking freezing"), **пизда́тый** (excellent), **пиздёж** (lies, bullshit), **пиздюли́** (a beating: дать пиздюле́й, получи́ть пиздюле́й) and the almost affectionate **распиздя́й** (a slacker, someone hopelessly disorganized). There are also two different verbs that learners mix up. **Пизде́ть** (он пизди́т) is to talk rubbish or to lie; **пи́здить** (он пи́здит) is to beat or, more often with a prefix, to steal: **спи́здить**.
- From **хуй**: **хуйня́** (nonsense; also any thing whose name you can't be bothered to find), **хуёвый** and **хуёво** (lousy, badly), **охуе́ть** (to be stunned, or to lose all restraint), **охуе́нный** (amazing).
- From **блядь**: mostly the bare word and its shortened form **бля**, used as an exclamation.

:::ex
Хва́тит пизде́ть. | Stop talking shit.
У меня́ телефо́н спи́здили. | Some bastard stole my phone.
Что э́то за хуйня́? | What the fuck is this?
Пого́да хуёвая. | The weather's shit.
:::

There are also a few all-purpose verbs, such as **хуя́рить** and **еба́шить**, that mean little more than "to do something with great force": to hit, to work flat out, to pour down, to drive fast. Context and prefix fill in the rest, as in **нахуя́риться**, to get very drunk.

## Мат in the slots of ordinary grammar

Several мат expressions are simply obscene versions of grammatical words you already know, and they take the same constructions.

**Negation and quantity.** Ни хуя́ replaces ничего́ and keeps the double negative. Дохуя́ replaces мно́го and keeps the genitive. **Не́хуй** replaces не́чего in the [не́чего + infinitive](#s-06-04) pattern, with the meaning "there's no reason to".

:::ex
Я ни хуя́ не слы́шу. | I can't hear a fucking thing.
Там бы́ло дохуя́ наро́ду. | There were a shitload of people there.
Не́хуй бы́ло опа́здывать. | You shouldn't have fucking been late. | compare Не́чего бы́ло опа́здывать.
:::

**Not caring.** По́хуй takes a dative, like every other word for how something affects you: мне по́хуй, ему́ по́хуй.

**Question words.** Како́го ху́я? is an angry "why?" or "what gives you the right?", на хуя́? is an angry "what for?", and **ху́ли** covers both "why the hell" and a shrugging "well, what do you expect".

:::ex
Како́го ху́я ты тут де́лаешь? | What the fuck are you doing here?
На хуя́ ты э́то купи́л? | What the fuck did you buy that for?
Ху́ли ты молчи́шь? | Why the fuck aren't you saying anything?
:::

**Emphatic "no".** Хуй at the front of a clause denies it flatly, with no не.

:::ex
Хуй ты угада́л. | Like hell you guessed right.
Хуй его́ зна́ет. | Fuck knows.
:::

**Dismissal and doom.** Three short fixed patterns: хуй с + instrumental waves something away, в пизду́ + accusative throws it out, and dative + пизда́ announces that someone is finished.

:::ex
Хуй с ним. | Fuck it. / Forget him.
Да в пизду́ э́ту рабо́ту. | Fuck this job.
Нам пизда́. | We're fucked.
:::

**Particles.** Блядь, usually shortened to бля, has worn down into a pure particle. It slots in after the first phrase of a sentence, set off by commas, just like the particles in [the particle sections](#s-07-02), and it adds nothing but heat. **На́хуй** tacked onto the end of a command does the same job.

:::ex
Ну ты, блядь, даёшь. | Well, fuck me, you're something else.
Закро́й дверь на́хуй. | Shut the fucking door.
:::

**The dismissive echo.** To pour scorn on a word, repeat it with its beginning replaced by ху-, the way English says "money, shmoney": де́ньги-хуе́ньги. The fixed phrase **хуё-моё**, "this, that and the other", comes from the same game.

## The fighting words

Most of the above is coloring. A few fixed phrases are different: they are real insults, aimed at a person, and saying one to a stranger can start a fight.

:::ex
Пошёл [[ns:на́ хуй]]! | Fuck off! / Go fuck yourself!
Иди́ в пизду́! | Fuck off!
Ёб твою́ мать! | literally "fucked your mother"
:::

The first is the standard hostile dismissal, and everything with **на́фиг**, **[[ns:на́ хрен]]** and **[[ns:на́ хер]]** is a softened copy of it. The third is the original "mother" curse. Today it is mostly an exclamation of dismay or amazement directed at nobody, like "oh, for fuck's sake".

Nouns for people work as insults in the obvious way: **муда́к** (an asshole), **долбоёб** (a fucking idiot), **уёбок** (a piece of scum), **блядь** said of a woman. The tone decides everything: between friends «ну ты муда́к» can be affectionate, and from a stranger it is a challenge.

Russian also has slurs aimed at ethnic groups and at gay people. Those are a different thing. Мат is obscene, which means it offends against the occasion; a slur attacks a person for what they are. This guide does not list them.

## How it looks in writing

Because the words are off limits in print, you will mostly meet them disguised.

- **Asterisks and dots:** х\*й, п\*\*\*\*ц, б\*\*\*ь, or just the first letter and dots.
- **Clipped forms in chats:** [[ns:нах]] and [[ns:пох]] for [[ns:на хуй]] and по́хуй, [[ns:хз]] for хуй зна́ет ("no idea"), [[ns:пздц]] and [[ns:ппц]] for пизде́ц and пипе́ц, мля for бля.
- **Блять:** the exclamation is very often spelled this way, with т. Many people feel it as a different, milder word from the noun блядь; dictionaries don't recognize the distinction.
- **The bleep:** on television and in most online video the words are beeped out or silenced, often with the first sound left in.

## Who says it, and where

**In public it is strictly off limits.** Laws in force since 2013 and 2014 forbid мат in films shown in cinemas, in theater, at concerts and in the media. Books and recordings that contain it are sold sealed, with a label reading «Соде́ржит нецензу́рную брань». Swearing in a public place is an administrative offense, classed as petty hooliganism, and can bring a fine or a short arrest. Enforcement is patchy, but the status is clear: these words do not appear in a newspaper or a prime-time interview. English has nothing this formal.

**In private it is ordinary.** Among friends, colleagues who know each other well, soldiers, builders, programmers, students and a great many other people, мат is simply how relaxed speech sounds, and for some speakers бля is punctuation. It signals closeness as often as anger: swearing in front of someone says "I'm not on my guard with you". Most of what you overhear on a Russian street is this kind, aimed at nobody.

**What governs it is company, not the words.** The traditional rule is that мат is not used in front of children, older relatives or strangers, at a family table, or in a formal setting; an older version of the rule says not in front of women, though plenty of women swear. The same person who swears freely with friends will switch it off completely when a grandmother walks in. Russians are very good at judging this, and getting it wrong reads as a lack of upbringing more than as aggression. The nearest English comparison is not to a slur but to "fuck" as it stood in the 1950s: known to everyone, used constantly by many, and unprintable.

This is also why people who learned Russian at home abroad often know surprisingly little мат. The family home is exactly the setting where it is not used, and emigrant families tend to keep a more careful register than people in Russia do.

**For a learner** the difficulty is judging the company, and a foreign accent raises the stakes: мат in non-native Russian tends to come out either comic or more aggressive than you meant. A sensible path is to understand everything, use блин and the фиг words freely among friends, and let Russian friends lead on the rest. When someone swears in front of you, take it as a sign of trust. When you have heard how a particular group talks for a while, you will know what you can say in it.

:::aside Where it comes from, and where it lives
A popular story says the Mongols brought мат to Russia. They did not. All four roots are native Slavic words with cousins across the Slavic languages, and several of them turn up in the birch-bark letters of medieval Novgorod and its neighbors, scratched by ordinary townspeople as early as the twelfth century.

It also has a long life in literature and music, usually just out of official sight. Пу́шкин used it in letters and epigrams; the eighteenth-century poet Барко́в is remembered for little else; Венеди́кт Ерофе́ев's «Москва́ — Петушки́» and much late Soviet underground writing depend on it. Today its best-known public voice is Серге́й Шнуро́в and his band «Ленингра́д», followed by most of Russian rap.
:::

:::summary
- Мат is four roots (хуй, пизда́, еба́ть, блядь) plus everything word formation can build from them.
- Its main job is intensity, not sex and not always insult; nearly every мат expression has a neutral twin.
- Each expression has weaker versions on the same frame: фиг, then хрен, then the real thing.
- The prefixes mean what they always mean: заеба́ть, наеба́ть, проеба́ть, отъеба́ться.
- It is banned from media and public performance and ordinary among friends. What matters is the company.
:::
