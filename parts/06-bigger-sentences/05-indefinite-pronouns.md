# Someone, anyone, something: -то, -нибудь, кое-, любо́й

English has two families of vague words, *some-* and *any-*, and chooses between them mostly by sentence type: "I saw **some**one" but "Did you see **any**one?" and "I didn't see **any**one."

Russian has more families, and it sorts them by a completely different question: **is there a particular one, and who knows which?** Sentence type has nothing to do with it directly. That's why translating *some-* and *any-* word for word goes wrong so fast — and why, once you have the Russian logic, the choice is nearly always obvious.

The machinery itself is trivial: take any question word and attach a particle.

:::vocab
кто-то, что-то | someone, something | a particular one, not identified
кто-[[ns:нибудь]], что-[[ns:нибудь]] | someone / anyone, something / anything | no particular one
[[ns:кое]]-что, [[ns:кое]]-кто | something, someone | I know which, I'm not saying; "a thing or two"
любо́й | any (at all), whichever |
ка́ждый | each, every |
вся́кий | all sorts of; any and every |
кто уго́дно, что уго́дно | anyone at all, anything at all |
не́которые | some, certain (ones) | pl.
не́сколько | several, a few | + gen. pl.
почему́-то | for some reason |
когда́-[[ns:нибудь]] | someday; ever |
когда́-то | once, at one time |
:::

## -то: there is one, I just can't tell you which

With **-то**, the speaker has a definite, real thing in mind — it exists, it happened — but can't name it (or doesn't bother to). Someone called: a real person, I just don't know who. I heard something: a real sound, I couldn't make it out.

:::ex
Тебе́ **кто-то** звони́л. | Someone called you. | a particular person; I don't know who
Я **что-то** слы́шал. | I heard something.
Она́ **куда́-то** ушла́. | She's gone off somewhere.
Мой телефо́н **где-то** здесь. | My phone is somewhere here.
**Кто-то** забы́л в авто́бусе зонт. | Somebody left an umbrella on the bus.
Он **почему́-то** не отвеча́ет. | For some reason he isn't answering.
Вчера́ я ви́дел тебя́ с **како́й-то** де́вушкой. | Yesterday I saw you with some girl.
Э́то **чья-то** су́мка. | This is somebody's bag.
:::

Because -то needs something real to point at, it lives mainly in statements about the past and the present.

## -нибудь: no particular one

With **-нибудь**, there is no specific thing in the speaker's mind. Maybe one will turn up, maybe not; and if it does, any one will do. It's the "whoever, whatever, I don't mind which" particle.

That naturally puts it in sentences about things that aren't settled facts yet:

**Questions** — you're asking whether there was one at all.

:::ex
Мне **кто-[[ns:нибудь]]** звони́л? | Did anyone call me?
Ты **что-[[ns:нибудь]]** ел сего́дня? | Have you eaten anything today?
Вы **когда́-[[ns:нибудь]]** бы́ли в Япо́нии? | Have you ever been to Japan?
У **кого́-[[ns:нибудь]]** есть заря́дка? | Does anyone have a charger?
:::

**Commands and requests** — any one will do; you choose.

:::ex
Купи́ **что-[[ns:нибудь]]** к ча́ю. | Buy something to go with the tea.
Спроси́ **кого́-[[ns:нибудь]]** ещё. | Ask someone else.
Расскажи́ **каку́ю-[[ns:нибудь]]** исто́рию. | Tell us a story — any story.
:::

**The future, wishes, conditions** — it hasn't happened, so there's no particular one yet.

:::ex
Дава́й **куда́-[[ns:нибудь]]** пойдём. | Let's go somewhere.
Я хочу́ **что-[[ns:нибудь]]** съесть. | I want to eat something.
**Когда́-[[ns:нибудь]]** я вы́учу кита́йский. | Someday I'll learn Chinese.
Е́сли **кто-[[ns:нибудь]]** позвони́т, скажи́, что я за́нят. | If anyone calls, say I'm busy.
Я бы **где-[[ns:нибудь]]** посиде́л. | I'd like to sit down somewhere.
:::

**Repeated situations** — a different one each time, so no single one to point to.

:::ex
По вечера́м он обы́чно **что-[[ns:нибудь]]** чита́ет. | In the evenings he usually reads something or other.
Ка́ждый раз **кто-[[ns:нибудь]]** опа́здывает. | Every time, somebody's late.
:::

## Side by side

The contrast is clearest when everything else is held the same.

:::ex
**Кто-то** пришёл. | Someone's here. | I heard the door
**Кто-[[ns:нибудь]]** пришёл? | Has anyone come? | I have no idea
Он **что-то** чита́ет. | He's reading something. | right now; one particular thing
Он всегда́ **что-[[ns:нибудь]]** чита́ет. | He's always reading something. | varies from day to day
Я **что-то** съел, и тепе́рь у меня́ боли́т живо́т. | I ate something, and now my stomach hurts. | a real, guilty item
Я хочу́ **что-[[ns:нибудь]]** съесть. | I want to eat something. | anything
Она́ **куда́-то** уе́хала. | She's gone away somewhere. | she's there now, wherever it is
Мне на́до **куда́-[[ns:нибудь]]** уе́хать. | I need to get away somewhere. | destination open
:::

Notice that it's not "statement versus question". A question can perfectly well use -то when the speaker does have a definite thing in mind:

:::ex
Тебе́ **кто-то** звони́л? | Did someone call you? | I heard your phone — I'm asking about that call
Ты **что-то** сказа́л? | Did you say something? | I think I heard you
Ты **что-[[ns:нибудь]]** сказа́л? | Did you say anything? | e.g. at the meeting — or did you stay silent?
:::

And a future sentence can use -то when the thing is already fixed, just unknown to you: За́втра к нам **кто-то** придёт — "Someone is coming to see us tomorrow" (it's arranged; I don't know who). With кто-[[ns:нибудь]] the same sentence would mean "surely *somebody* will come".

:::note A quick test
Try adding "— I don't know which" and "— any, it doesn't matter which". If the first fits, you want **-то**. If the second fits, you want **-нибудь**.
:::

## Forms

Every question word takes both particles:

| | -то | -нибудь |
|---|---|---|
| кто | кто-то | кто-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| что | что-то | что-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| како́й | како́й-то | како́й-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| чей | чей-то | чей-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| где | где-то | где-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| куда́ | куда́-то | куда́-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| отку́да | отку́да-то | отку́да-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| когда́ | когда́-то | когда́-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| как | как-то | как-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| почему́ | почему́-то | почему́-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| заче́м | заче́м-то | заче́м-[[ns:нибудь]] |
| ско́лько | ско́лько-то | ско́лько-[[ns:нибудь]] |

The particle is glued on with a hyphen and never changes. **The question word declines as usual**, and a preposition simply goes in front of the whole thing. The stress stays on the question word; -то and -нибудь are never stressed.

:::ex
Я **кого́-то** ви́дел у две́ри. | I saw someone at the door. | acc.
Он **кому́-то** звони́т. | He's calling someone. | dat.
Она́ **с кем-то** разгова́ривает. | She's talking with someone. | instr.
Они́ **о чём-то** спо́рят. | They're arguing about something. | prep.
Дай мне **каку́ю-[[ns:нибудь]]** ру́чку. | Give me a pen, any pen. | f. acc.
Поговори́ **с кем-[[ns:нибудь]]** из колле́г. | Talk to one of your coworkers. | instr.
Спроси́ **у кого́-[[ns:нибудь]]** доро́гу. | Ask somebody the way. | gen.
:::

## Some words with a life of their own

A few of these combinations have picked up extra meanings worth knowing.

**Когда́-то** is "once, at one time" — some real moment in the past. **Когда́-[[ns:нибудь]]** is "someday" in the future and "ever" in questions.

:::ex
**Когда́-то** здесь был кинотеа́тр. | There used to be a movie theater here once.
**Когда́-[[ns:нибудь]]** ты меня́ поймёшь. | Someday you'll understand me.
:::

**Как-то** is "somehow" — and also "one time", especially as **как-то раз**. **Как-[[ns:нибудь]]** is "somehow or other, any old way" and "sometime" in loose invitations.

:::ex
Он **как-то** узна́л мой а́дрес. | He found out my address somehow.
**Как-то раз** мы заблуди́лись в лесу́. | One time we got lost in the woods.
Не волну́йся, **как-[[ns:нибудь]]** спра́вимся. | Don't worry, we'll manage somehow.
Заходи́ **как-[[ns:нибудь]]**! | Drop by sometime!
:::

**Что-то** at the start of a remark often means "somehow, for some reason" — a mild puzzlement about your own state or the situation.

:::ex
**Что-то** я сего́дня уста́л. | I'm kind of tired today, for some reason.
**Что-то** он до́лго не идёт. | He's taking a long time, isn't he.
:::

**Како́й-то** before a noun is "some… or other", often dismissive — and before a name it means "some person called…". With an adjective it's "sort of".

:::ex
Тебе́ звони́л **како́й-то** Ива́н. | Some Ivan called you.
Он сего́дня **како́й-то** стра́нный. | He's kind of strange today.
:::

**Где-то** also means "approximately": **где-то** в пять — "around five"; **где-то** ты́сяча рубле́й — "a thousand rubles or so".

:::note -либо
There's a third particle, **-либо**, which means the same as -нибудь and belongs to formal writing: каки́е-[[ns:либо]] вопро́сы ("any questions"), кто-[[ns:либо]], когда́-[[ns:либо]]. In speech you'll mostly meet it in one frame — after a superlative: лу́чший фильм, кото́рый я **когда́-[[ns:либо]]** ви́дел, "the best film I've ever seen".
:::

## Кое-: I know, but I'm not saying

The prefix **кое-** turns things around. With -то the speaker *doesn't* know which; with кое- the speaker knows perfectly well and is choosing not to spell it out. It is the pronoun of hints, surprises and discretion.

:::ex
Мне на́до тебе́ **[[ns:кое]]-что** сказа́ть. | I need to tell you something.
Я сего́дня **[[ns:кое]]-кого́** встре́тил. Угада́й кого́! | I ran into someone today. Guess who!
У меня́ для тебя́ **[[ns:кое]]-что** есть. | I've got a little something for you.
**[[ns:Кое]]-кто** опя́ть не помы́л посу́ду. | *Someone* didn't do the dishes again. | pointed
:::

Its second meaning is "a few, some (but not much)": **[[ns:кое]]-что** — "a thing or two", **[[ns:кое]]-где** — "here and there", **[[ns:кое]]-каки́е** — "a few, certain", **[[ns:кое]]-когда́** — "now and then".

:::ex
Я **[[ns:кое]]-что** зна́ю об э́том. | I know a thing or two about that.
**[[ns:Кое]]-где** ещё лежи́т снег. | There's still snow here and there.
У меня́ есть **[[ns:кое]]-каки́е** иде́и. | I have a few ideas.
:::

**[[ns:Кое]]-как** has gone its own way: it means "sloppily, any old how" or "just barely".

:::ex
Он сде́лал рабо́ту **[[ns:кое]]-как**. | He did the job any old how.
Мы **[[ns:кое]]-как** добрали́сь до до́ма. | We barely made it home.
:::

Unlike -то and -нибудь, кое- is split by a preposition, which lands in the middle (no hyphens then): **[[ns:кое]] с кем**, **[[ns:кое]] о чём**, **[[ns:кое]] у кого́**.

:::ex
Мне ну́жно **[[ns:кое]] с кем** поговори́ть. | I need to have a word with someone.
Я хоте́л тебя́ **[[ns:кое]] о чём** попроси́ть. | I wanted to ask you for something.
:::

Pronunciation: [[ns:кое]] is said [[ns:ко́е]], with a light stress of its own, but the main stress of the combination falls on the pronoun.

## "Any": three different things

English "any" translates three ways, depending on what it's doing.

**1. "Any" in a question or condition** — that's **-нибудь**, as above: Есть каки́е-[[ns:нибудь]] но́вости? — "Any news?"

**2. "Any" after a negative** — that's the **ни-** series with не, from [the previous section](#s-06-04). Don't use -нибудь here.

:::ex
Я **никого́ не** ви́дел. | I didn't see anyone.
Он **ничего́ не** сказа́л. | He didn't say anything.
:::

**3. "Any at all — take your pick"** — that's **любо́й**, or a question word + **уго́дно**.

Любо́й is an adjective (like молодо́й). Alone, it means "anyone".

:::ex
Приходи́ в **любо́е** вре́мя. | Come any time.
Вы́бери **любу́ю** ка́рту. | Pick any card.
Э́то зна́ет **любо́й** шко́льник. | Any schoolkid knows that.
Спроси́ **любо́го** — тебе́ ска́жут то же са́мое. | Ask anyone — they'll tell you the same thing.
:::

**Уго́дно** attaches (as a separate word) to any question word, with the feel of "…you like, no limits". Prepositions go before the question word.

:::ex
Здесь мо́жно купи́ть **что уго́дно**. | You can buy anything at all here.
Э́то мо́жет случи́ться **с кем уго́дно**. | That can happen to anybody.
Он мо́жет спать **где уго́дно**. | He can sleep anywhere.
Бери́ **ско́лько уго́дно**. | Take as much as you like.
Звони́ **когда́ уго́дно**. | Call whenever.
:::

## Любо́й, ка́ждый, вся́кий

These three crowd around English "any" and "every", so let's separate them.

**Ка́ждый** is "each, every": all members of a set, taken one at a time. Nobody is left out.

**Любо́й** is "any": pick one, whichever you like; the choice makes no difference.

:::ex
Я хожу́ в спортза́л **ка́ждый** день. | I go to the gym every day.
Приходи́ в **любо́й** день. | Come any day. | one day, you choose
**Ка́ждый** студе́нт получи́л зада́ние. | Every student got an assignment.
**Любо́й** студе́нт мо́жет отве́тить на э́тот вопро́с. | Any student can answer this question.
:::

Приходи́ ка́ждый день would be an invitation to show up daily — a rather different offer.

**Вся́кий** is "of every kind, all sorts of". Its typical use is the plural, slightly dismissive: вся́кие лю́ди — "all kinds of people", вся́кая ерунда́ — "all sorts of nonsense". As "anyone, everyone" (вся́кий зна́ет) it sounds old-fashioned. But it sits in several fixed phrases you'll use daily:

:::ex
В метро́ е́здят **вся́кие** лю́ди. | All kinds of people ride the metro.
Он чита́ет **вся́кую** ерунду́. | He reads all sorts of rubbish.
Возьми́ зонт **на вся́кий слу́чай**. | Take an umbrella just in case.
**Вся́кое** быва́ет. | Anything can happen. Stuff happens.
Она́ согласи́лась **без вся́ких** вопро́сов. | She agreed without any questions.
:::

## Certain ones, several, and "a"

**Не́которые** (plural) is "some" in the sense of "certain ones, part of the total" — the "some" that is opposed to "others" or "all".

:::ex
**Не́которые** лю́ди не едя́т мя́са. | Some people don't eat meat.
**Не́которые из** мои́х друзе́й уже́ жена́ты. | Some of my friends are already married.
**Не́которым** э́то нра́вится. | Some people like it.
:::

**Не́сколько** + genitive plural is "several, a few": не́сколько дней, не́сколько раз, не́сколько челове́к.

**Оди́н** — the number "one" — is Russian's everyday way to say "a certain", when you're introducing someone or something specific that the listener doesn't know yet. It's often the best translation of English "a… of mine" or "this (guy)".

:::ex
**Оди́н** мой друг рабо́тает в Гу́гле. | A friend of mine works at Google.
Мне э́то рассказа́ла **одна́** знако́мая. | A woman I know told me that.
Я зна́ю **одно́** хоро́шее ме́сто недалеко́ отсю́да. | I know a good place not far from here.
**Одни́** лю́бят ко́фе, **други́е** — чай. | Some like coffee, others tea.
:::

In formal or ironic writing the same job is done by **не́кий** ("a certain"): не́кий господи́н Ивано́в. And the literary words **не́кто** ("a certain someone") and **не́что** ("something") — don't confuse them with не́кого and не́чего — mostly appear in the pattern не́что + adjective: не́что стра́нное, "something strange".

:::dialogue
А́ня: Мне кто-[[ns:нибудь]] звони́л? | Did anyone call me?
Ди́ма: Да, кто-то звони́л, но не сказа́л, кто он. | Yes, someone called but didn't say who he was.
А́ня: Он что-[[ns:нибудь]] проси́л переда́ть? | Did he leave any message?
Ди́ма: Нет, ничего́. Сказа́л, что перезвони́т как-[[ns:нибудь]] по́зже. | No, nothing. He said he'd call back sometime later.
А́ня: Стра́нно. Ла́дно. Слу́шай, мне на́до тебе́ [[ns:кое]]-что рассказа́ть… | Strange. OK. Listen, I've got something to tell you…
:::

:::summary
- **-то**: a particular one exists; the speaker can't or won't identify it.
- **-нибудь**: no particular one; any will do, or it's not established that there is one (questions, commands, future, conditions, habits).
- **кое-**: the speaker knows which and isn't saying; also "a few".
- After a negative: **ни-** + не. Free choice: **любо́й**, **кто / что / где уго́дно**.
- **ка́ждый** = every one of them; **любо́й** = any one you pick; **вся́кий** = all sorts of.
:::
