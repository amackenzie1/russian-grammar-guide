# Stress and vowel reduction

Every Russian word of more than one syllable has one syllable that dominates: it is louder, noticeably longer, and its vowel is pronounced clearly. That is the **stressed** syllable. The other syllables are weaker, and their vowels get blurred. This blurring, called **vowel reduction**, is why хорошо́ (good, fine) doesn't sound like *kho-ro-SHO*.

English does the same thing: in "banana" only the middle *a* is a real *a*, and the other two are a neutral "uh".

The hard part: Russian stress is unpredictable, it moves between the forms of a word, and it is not written.

## Stress can fall anywhere

Russian has no fixed place for stress, as Czech or Polish do. It can land on any syllable, and the spelling doesn't show which.

:::ex
соба́ка | dog | middle
молоко́ | milk | last
ко́мната | room | first
учи́тельница | teacher (f.) | second of five
:::

Borrowed words and names are no safer; Russian often took them from French or German:

:::ex
телефо́н | phone
компью́тер | computer
шокола́д | chocolate
Флори́да | Florida
:::

Likewise Влади́мир, Бори́с, Ива́н, Набо́ков and Шара́пова.

## Stress can change the word

Two words can differ only in stress.

:::ex
за́мок — замо́к | castle — lock
мука́ — му́ка | flour — torment
писа́ть — пи́сать | to write — to pee
уже́ — у́же | already — narrower
до́ма — дома́ | of a house; at home — houses
сто́ит — стои́т | costs; is worth — is standing
пла́чу — плачу́ | I'm crying — I'm paying
ду́хи — духи́ | spirits, ghosts — perfume
:::

Stress also separates forms of one word. Дом is "house"; до́ма is both its genitive form, "of a house", and the adverb "at home", while дома́ is the plural, "houses". Ordinary Russian text prints no marks, so [[ns: Я плачу]] could be "I'm crying" or "I'm paying". Russians tell them apart from context.

## Stress moves

Many words keep their stress in every form. Many of the most common ones don't.

:::ex
рука́ → ру́ки | hand → hands
го́род → города́ | city → cities
пишу́ → пи́шешь | I write → you write
был, была́, бы́ли | was (m.), was (f.), were
:::

The shifts follow a limited number of patterns, collected in [Stress patterns](#s-09-07); later sections point them out as they come up. So knowing the stress of the dictionary form is necessary but not always sufficient.

## What happens to unstressed vowels

Away from the stress, Russian vowels collapse into fewer sounds. The sounds written у/ю, ы and и stay recognizably themselves, only shorter. The others change.

From here on, the Latin spellings show the actual pronunciation, reduction included. *Uh* is the neutral vowel at the end of English "sofa".

### Unstressed о and а

Unstressed о is not pronounced *o* (the few exceptions are noted below). It merges with а, and the result depends on its distance from the stress:

- **In the syllable right before the stress**, and at the very start of a word, both о and а are a short, clear *a*.
- **Everywhere else** (two or more syllables before the stress, or anywhere after it), both become the weak *uh*.

:::ex
окно́ | window | *ak-NO*
спаси́бо | thank you | *spa-SEE-buh*
рабо́та | work, job | *ra-BO-tuh*
пого́да | weather | *pa-GO-duh*
молоко́ | milk | *muh-la-KO*
хорошо́ | good, fine | *khuh-ra-SHO*
голова́ | head | *guh-la-VA*
магази́н | store | *muh-ga-ZEEN*
ко́мната | room | *KOM-nuh-tuh*
:::

Молоко́ has three letters о and three different sounds: *uh*, then *a* just before the stress, then a real *o* under it. Хорошо́ follows the same pattern, *uh-a-STRESS*.

The spelling never reflects this. To pronounce a written word you must know its stress. To spell a word you hear, you must know whether its unstressed *a* or *uh* is о or а. Russian schoolchildren are taught a check: find a form where the stress falls on the doubtful vowel. Вода́ (water) is *va-DA*, but its plural во́ды is *VO-dy*, so the letter is о.

### Unstressed е and я

After a soft consonant, unstressed е and я are both pronounced as a short, lax *i*, close to the vowel of English "bit". So is а after ч and щ in a syllable before the stress. It is written here as *ee*, but keep it short.

:::ex
сестра́ | sister | *sees-TRA*
телефо́н | phone | *tee-lee-FON*
ме́сяц | month | *MYE-seets*
пяти́ | of five | *pee-TEE*
часы́ | clock, watch | *chee-SY*
:::

At the start of a word, where these letters include a *y* sound, the vowel part does the same:

:::ex
язы́к | language | *yee-ZYK*
ещё | still, more | *yee-SHCHO*
еда́ | food | *yee-DA*
:::

So пять (five) has a clear *a*, but пяти́ is *pee-TEE*. An unstressed *ee* could be written и, е or я; the spelling has to be learned with the word.

At the very end of a word, unstressed я relaxes further, toward a weak *yuh*: неде́ля (week) is roughly *nee-DYE-lyuh*, и́мя (name) is *EE-myuh*.

### After ж, ш and ц

These three consonants are always hard, so unstressed е after them sounds like a short ы, not the *i* of "bit":

:::ex
жена́ | wife | *zhy-NA*
цена́ | price | *tsy-NA*
шепта́ть | to whisper | *shyp-TAT'*
:::

In a handful of words, а after ж or ш does the same in the syllable before the stress: к сожале́нию (unfortunately) is *k suh-zhy-LYE-nee-yu*.

### A summary table

| Written | Under stress | Right before the stress | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|
| а | *a* | *a* | *uh* |
| о | *o* | *a* | *uh* |
| е (after a soft consonant) | *e* | short *i* | short *i* |
| я (after a soft consonant) | *a* | short *i* | short *i*, weak *yuh* at the end of a word |
| е (after ж, ш, ц) | *e* | short ы | short ы |
| и, ы, у, ю | themselves | themselves, shorter | themselves, shorter |

:::note Aim for the rhythm
Reduction is a matter of rhythm, and a Russian word has three levels, not two. Give the word one strong syllable. Keep the syllable right before it clear and unhurried. Pass lightly over the remaining syllables only. Молоко́ is *muh-la-KO*: light, clear, strong. Pronouncing every о as *o* sounds stilted, and so does rushing every unstressed syllable equally.
:::

:::aside Places where о stays о
Borrowed words that end in a vowel plus о (-ио, -ео, -ао, -уо) keep a clearly audible final о in ordinary speech: ра́дио (radio), ви́део (video), а́удио (audio), кака́о (cocoa). Reducing that final vowel sounds foreign. And some regions of northern Russia traditionally pronounce every о as *o*, a feature Russians call о́канье and hear as rustic. The standard language, based on Moscow speech, reduces.
:::

## Ё is always stressed

The letter ё is always a stressed vowel, and in all but a few long compound words it carries the main stress. This is why it never takes a stress mark in this guide: the two dots already tell you. When stress moves away, ё can't survive, and е appears in its place:

:::ex
сёстры → сестра́ | sisters → sister
жёны → жена́ | wives → wife
:::

## Little words that lean on their neighbors

Short function words, above all prepositions and the negative не, normally have no stress of their own. They join the following word, and their vowels reduce accordingly: не зна́ю (I don't know) is one unit, *nee-ZNA-yu*; до свида́ния (goodbye) is *duh-svee-DA-nee-yuh*.

In a small number of fixed combinations the little word takes the stress and its neighbor is left with none: [[ns: не́ был]] (wasn't, *NYE-byl*), [[ns: за́ город]] (out of town, *ZA-guh-rut*). These are set phrases.

## How this guide marks stress

Every Russian word of two or more syllables in this guide carries an acute accent over its stressed vowel: молоко́, соба́ка. One-syllable words (дом, я, нет) and words with ё go unmarked.

The marks are a learner's aid, not part of Russian spelling; don't write them in your own messages. Where they do appear in the real world is covered in [the last section of this part](#s-01-06).

## How to learn stress

No rule replaces knowing the word, so learn each word together with its stress. Dictionaries mark it, and good ones show it for every form of the word.

Roots are unpredictable, but many endings and suffixes are reliable:

- Words with ё are stressed on the ё.
- Adjectives whose dictionary form ends in -ой are stressed on that ending: большо́й (big), молодо́й (young), плохо́й (bad). Those in -ый and -ий are stressed somewhere on the stem: но́вый (new), ма́ленький (small).
- Nouns in -а́ция are stressed on the а: информа́ция, ситуа́ция.
- Nouns in -и́ст and -и́зм are stressed on the suffix: тури́ст, журнали́ст, тури́зм.
- Verbs that mean a completed action and start with the prefix вы- are stressed on it: вы́пить (to drink up), вы́учить (to learn by heart).

Russians argue about stress too. The standard form is звони́т (he/she is calling), but a huge number of people say зво́нит, and others wince when they hear it. Тво́рог and творо́г (a kind of fresh curd cheese) are both officially accepted.
